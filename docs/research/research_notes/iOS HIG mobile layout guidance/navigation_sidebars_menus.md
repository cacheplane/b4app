# iOS HIG mobile layout guidance: navigation, sidebars, toolbars, search, menus, sheets (iOS 26 / Liquid Glass)

Context: a SwiftUI app built on `NavigationSplitView`. The sidebar has "New thread", "Search" and "More" rows, a thread list, and a profile/account button at the bottom. The detail is a chat transcript with a toolbar and a composer. All HIG pages were read through the JSON endpoints (`developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/<page>.json`) on 2026-11-01.

Revision note: several HIG pages carry change-log entries dated after the iOS 26 launch (December 16, 2025 "Updated guidance for Liquid Glass"; March 24, 2026 sheets; June 8, 2026 sidebars, menus, search fields and tab bars; September 9, 2026 layout). The June 2026 entries come from the WWDC26 cycle, so they may describe the next OS release rather than iOS 26 itself. Each one is flagged where it applies.

## 1. Sidebars on iPhone, and how NavigationSplitView collapses on compact width

### Takeaway
Apple says to prefer split views in regular width, not compact, and to consider a tab bar before a sidebar. On iPhone, `NavigationSplitView` collapses into a single `NavigationStack` and shows "the last column that displays useful information". `preferredCompactColumn` lets the app choose which column sits on top (for example, open straight into the transcript, with the sidebar reached through Back). The sidebar's rows then become an ordinary pushed list, with chevrons on `NavigationLink` rows.

### Cited Findings
- HIG Split views, iOS: "Prefer using a split view in a regular — not a compact — environment." Reason given: "In a compact environment, such as iPhone in portrait orientation, it's difficult to display multiple panes without wrapping or truncating the content." The iOS/iPadOS considerations were added June 9, 2025. — [HIG Split views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/split-views)
- HIG Split views: "persistently highlight the current selection in each pane that leads to the detail view." The page gives no explicit iPhone collapse guidance and defers to Layout and the developer docs. — [HIG Split views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/split-views)
- HIG Sidebars: a sidebar "requires a large amount of vertical and horizontal space. When space is limited... a more compact control such as a tab bar may work better." On iOS and iPadOS: "Consider a tab bar first." Use `TabViewStyle.sidebarAdaptable` for an app that switches between the two, or `NavigationSplitView` for a sidebar only. The page has no iPhone-specific statement such as "on iPhone a sidebar becomes a list". — [HIG Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)
- HIG Sidebars: "Show no more than two levels of hierarchy." Use disclosure controls to group large hierarchies. "Avoid hiding the sidebar by default." On macOS (not iOS): "Avoid critical information or actions at the bottom of the sidebar." Change log: June 8, 2026, "clarified guidance for the adaptable sidebar style" (WWDC26 cycle); June 9, 2025, extending content beneath the Liquid Glass sidebar. — [HIG Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)
- HIG Tab bars: "Use a tab bar to support navigation, not to provide actions... If you need to provide controls that act on elements in the current view, use a toolbar instead." Also: "Prefer a tab bar for navigation. If your app is more complex, you can provide the option to convert the tab bar to a sidebar." Use `NavigationSplitView` for a sidebar that never converts. The tab bar floats on Liquid Glass and can minimize on scroll. "A tab bar can include a dedicated search tab at the trailing end." — [HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- HIG Layout: "Determine layout based on size classes, not device type or orientation." "Don't change your app's functionality based on the space it occupies." "Consider taking advantage of larger spaces to switch from a tab bar to a sidebar or expose functionality that might otherwise be grouped into an overflow menu." The iOS platform section says "No additional considerations". — [HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- SwiftUI `NavigationSplitView`: "At narrow size classes, such as on iPhone or Apple Watch, a navigation split view collapses into a single stack. Typically SwiftUI automatically chooses the view to show on top of this single stack." The docs add that it "shows the last column that displays useful information," and "For rows in a list that have NavigationLink instances, the list draws disclosure chevrons while in the collapsed state." — [NavigationSplitView docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview)
- SwiftUI: "The split view ignores the visibility control when it collapses its columns into a stack," so `columnVisibility` has no effect on iPhone. — [NavigationSplitView docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview)
- SwiftUI `preferredCompactColumn`: pass a `Binding<NavigationSplitViewColumn>` to `init(preferredCompactColumn:sidebar:detail:)`. In Apple's example, `.detail` makes iPhone open on the detail view. When the person taps Back they see the sidebar, and the binding changes from `.detail` to `.sidebar`. — [NavigationSplitView docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview)
- SwiftUI: a `NavigationLink` in an earlier column "sets the view that the stack displays over its root view"; a link in the same column pushes onto that stack. Either way the link needs a matching `navigationDestination(for:)`. — [NavigationSplitView docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview)
- WWDC25 session 323: `NavigationSplitView` "now [has] a Liquid Glass sidebar that floats above your content." — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)

### Inferences
- On iPhone the sidebar column becomes the root screen of the stack. The rows "New thread", "Search" and "More" would render as plain list rows above the thread list, and that is not where iPhone users expect actions. Two HIG rules point the same way: actions belong in toolbars, and a list or tab bar is for navigation. So on compact width "New thread" and "Search" should move into the root screen's toolbar (bottom bar or nav bar), not stay as rows.
- For a chat app, `preferredCompactColumn` can open straight into the last thread, as Messages does, or into the list. Either way, keep the binding in sync with selection: when selection is cleared, go back to `.sidebar`.
- The macOS rule "avoid critical actions at the bottom of the sidebar" does not apply to iOS directly. But a profile button pinned to the bottom of the collapsed root list would scroll with the list or need a safe-area inset, and it would compete with the iOS 26 bottom toolbar. The nav bar is the more conventional place (see section 3).

### Gaps
- None of the HIG pages describes what a sidebar "becomes" on iPhone. The collapse behavior is documented only in the SwiftUI docs.
- I found no availability statement specific to `preferredCompactColumn`. It is commonly cited as iOS 17+, but I did not verify that here.

## 2. Navigation bars and toolbars on iOS 26: titles, item counts, grouping, ToolbarSpacer, glass, bottom bar

### Takeaway
In iOS 26, toolbar items sit on a floating Liquid Glass surface and the system groups them automatically. Apple's rules: put only the most important items in the bar and the rest in a More menu; use at most about three groups; prefer borderless SF Symbols over text (except for things like Edit); keep text buttons out of groups with symbol buttons; have only one `.prominent` primary action, on the trailing side; and remove custom bar backgrounds in favor of the scroll edge effect. `ToolbarSpacer(.fixed/.flexible, placement:)` (iOS 26+) is how you split groups.

### Cited Findings
- HIG Toolbars, iOS: "Prioritize only the most important items for inclusion in the main toolbar area." "Create a More menu to include additional items." "Use a large title to help people stay oriented as they navigate and scroll." The large title shrinks to a standard title on scroll (`UINavigationBar.prefersLargeTitles`). — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars: "In iOS, a navigation-specific toolbar is sometimes called a navigation bar." Navigation-bar guidance was folded into the Toolbars page on June 9, 2025. Liquid Glass guidance was updated December 16, 2025. — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, titles: "Write a concise title." Aim for "under 15 characters long." "Don't title windows with your app name." — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, crowding: "Choose items deliberately to avoid overcrowding." On the More menu: "Try to include all actions in the toolbar if possible, and only add this menu if you really need it." — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, locations: the leading edge holds Back and sidebar toggle, then the title. The center holds common controls, which collapse into overflow. "Items on the trailing edge remain visible at all window sizes": important items, search, the More menu, and the primary action. — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, grouping: "Group toolbar items logically by function and frequency of use." "Minimize the number of groups. In general, aim for a maximum of three." "Group navigation controls and critical actions like Done, Close, or Save in dedicated... visually distinct sections." — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, symbols vs. text: "Prefer simple, recognizable symbols for items instead of text, except for actions like edit that aren't well-represented by symbols." "Prefer system-provided symbols without borders." "Keep actions with text labels separate," using fixed space between them. — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, primary action: "Use the `.prominent` style for key actions such as Done or Submit. Only specify one primary action, and put it on the trailing side of the toolbar." — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, Back and Close: "Use the standard Back and Close buttons... don't use a text label that says Back or Close." — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- HIG Toolbars, backgrounds: "Reduce the use of toolbar backgrounds and tinted controls." Use `ScrollEdgeEffectStyle` "when necessary to distinguish the toolbar area from the content area." "Prefer using standard components in a toolbar" so corner radii stay concentric. — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- WWDC25 356: "Group bar items by function and frequency." "Be sure to not group symbols with text, like 'Select' and the share icon... since it could be perceived as a single button." "If your bar is feeling too crowded... move secondary actions into a more menu." "A primary action, like Done, stays separate and appears tinted, often as a blue checkmark on iOS." — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- WWDC25 356, scroll edge effect: "Soft is the default and the one you'll use in most cases, especially on iOS." "Apply one scroll edge effect per view." The effect "shouldn't be used where there aren't any floating UI elements." — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- WWDC25 323: "toolbar items are placed on a Liquid Glass surface that floats above your app's content... Toolbar items are automatically grouped." Use `ToolbarSpacer(.fixed)` to split groups and `ToolbarSpacer(.flexible)` to push them apart. `.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden)` "separate[s] an item into its own group without a background". Apple's example of this is a `ProfileButton()`. Toolbar items take `.badge(_:)`. "Icons use monochrome rendering in more places, including in toolbars." Tint only "to convey meaning." For extra backgrounds behind bar items: "make sure to remove them." — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- SwiftUI `ToolbarSpacer`: iOS 26.0+. `init(_ sizing: SpacerSizing, placement: ToolbarItemPlacement)`. "Spacers can have a standard fixed size or be flexible and push items apart." — [ToolbarSpacer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/toolbarspacer)
- WWDC25 323 gives a Mail-style bottom bar (`.bottomBar`) as sample code, in this order: `FilterPicker` | `ToolbarSpacer(.flexible)` | `DefaultToolbarItem(kind: .search)` | `ToolbarSpacer(.fixed)` | `NewMessageButton`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)

### Inferences
- A suggested transcript (detail) nav bar on iPhone: Back on the leading edge, then a short thread title (under about 15 characters, truncated), then at most one or two trailing symbol items (for example an info or inspector button and a More `Menu`). Each group should be function-based, with no more than three groups. The composer already acts as the bottom control, so the detail screen should probably not also have a `.bottomBar`.
- A suggested root (thread list) screen on iPhone: a large title. In the bottom bar, an optional filter on the leading side, search in the middle, and a "New thread" compose button on the trailing side, following Apple's sample code for Mail. Account/profile is a nav-bar item.
- Using a text label such as "Edit" is fine, but put it in its own group, separate from symbol buttons.

### Gaps
- HIG gives no numeric item count for iPhone. It says "most important items", "aim for a maximum of three [groups]", and (WWDC26 292) collapse search into a button "if you need more than two other toolbar items".
- I found no HIG point sizes for toolbar items in iOS 26. The HIG says to use the standard components; Apple Design Resources has the specs. Not fetched.

## 3. Search on iPhone in iOS 26 (and where New, Filter and Account go)

### Takeaway
On iPhone, search goes at the bottom when there's room ("Place search at the bottom if there's room"), and goes in the top bar only when the bottom is occupied or there is no bottom toolbar. `.searchable` applied to a `NavigationSplitView` puts the field at the top trailing on iPad and Mac, and iOS moves it to the bottom automatically on iPhone. `.searchToolbarBehavior(.minimize)` shows it as a button. `DefaultToolbarItem(kind: .search, placement: .bottomBar)` places it among other bottom items. Compose/New goes on the trailing side of that same bottom bar, as in Mail. HIG gives no guidance on account button placement.

### Cited Findings
- HIG Search fields, iOS: three entry points. Search can be a tab in a tab bar, sit in a toolbar at the bottom or top, or appear inline with content. Bottom: "Place search at the bottom if there's room." It "can share a toolbar with other items or be the only item" (Settings has it alone; Mail and Notes share it with other controls). Tapping it "animates into a field above the keyboard." Top: "Place search at the top when it's important to defer to content at the bottom of the screen, or there's no bottom toolbar" (Wallet). Inline: "When at the top, position an inline search field above the list it searches, and consider pinning it to the top toolbar when scrolling." Change log: June 9, 2025, updated iOS placement; June 8, 2026, refined guidance on search as a tab (WWDC26 cycle). — [HIG Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)
- HIG Search fields, general: "Use placeholder text to help people know what they can search for." "If possible, start search immediately when a person types." "Consider showing suggested search terms" (recent and predictive). Use a scope bar for "clearly defined search categories"; "Default to a broader scope." Tokens filter by common terms or items. — [HIG Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)
- HIG Search fields, iPad/Mac: "Put a search field at the trailing side of the toolbar for many common uses," which suits split views like Mail and Notes. Sidebar search suits filtering the sidebar itself. "In compact views, keep search where it is most contextually useful. Notes and Mail place it above the content list column." — [HIG Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)
- WWDC25 323: "Search in the toolbar places the field at the bottom of the screen, within easy reach." Applying `.searchable` to the `NavigationSplitView` "indicates that search applies to the entire NavigationSplitView," and on iPhone "this variant automatically adapts to bring the search field at the bottom of the display." "The system may choose to minimize the search field into a toolbar button." Opt in with `.searchToolbarBehavior(.minimize)` "because search isn't a main part of your app's experience." For a search tab, use `Tab(role: .search)` with `.searchable` on the `TabView`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- SwiftUI `searchToolbarBehavior(_:)`: iOS 26.0+. "On iPhone, the search field in the bottom toolbar can be configured to appear as a button-like control when inactive." Place it after `searchable`. — [searchToolbarBehavior docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/searchtoolbarbehavior(_:))
- `SearchToolbarBehavior` cases: `.automatic` and `.minimize` ("prefers rendering a search field as a button-like control"). The `searchToolbarBehavior(_:)` page's own code example uses `.minimized`, which conflicts with the type page and the WWDC25 session, both of which say `.minimize`. — [SearchToolbarBehavior docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/searchtoolbarbehavior); conflicting example in [searchToolbarBehavior docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/searchtoolbarbehavior(_:))
- SwiftUI `DefaultToolbarItem`: iOS 26.0+. "Place this item in your toolbar to control where the system-provided item, like search, will be positioned." Initializer: `init(kind: ToolbarDefaultItemKind, placement: ToolbarItemPlacement)`. — [DefaultToolbarItem docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/defaulttoolbaritem)
- WWDC26 292 "Design intuitive search experiences" (2026; flag: possibly next-OS era, though the guidance matches iOS 26): "While the bottom toolbar position is preferred, Search can also be placed in a Top Toolbar." The Mail example is "the most ergonomic position, and it's adjacent to other primary actions in the app"; "the width of the field automatically adapts. Accommodating both a leading, and a trailing action." "If you need more than two other toolbar items, search can also start as a button." Two questions decide placement: "how are people navigating my app?" and "what is the scope of my Search?" — [WWDC26 292](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/292/)
- Mail's compose button moved to the bottom-right in iOS 26 (secondary, press report; not Apple). Apple's own WWDC25 sample bottom bar ends with `NewMessageButton` on the trailing side, which supports the same layout. — [Pipedot / press](https://pipedot.org/6Y1ZG); [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- Accounts: HIG "Managing accounts" covers account deletion ("Provide a clear way to initiate account deletion within your app") and TV-provider sign-out. It gives no guidance on where profile or account access belongs. — [HIG Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- Apple's WWDC25 sample code places a `ProfileButton()` as a `ToolbarItem` with `.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden)`, so it is its own unglassed group. That suits an avatar image in the toolbar. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)

### Inferences
- In this app on iPhone, the root (sidebar) screen's "Search" row should become `.searchable` on the `NavigationSplitView`, which iOS puts at the bottom. "New thread" should be a trailing compose symbol (for example `square.and.pencil`) in `.bottomBar` next to search, laid out like the Mail sample. A filter, if there is one, can go on the leading side of the bottom bar or in a More `Menu`. "More" should become a trailing nav-bar `Menu` (ellipsis), and only if its actions can't sit in the toolbar.
- The profile/account control fits best as a top-bar avatar button (`.topBarLeading` or `.topBarTrailing`) with `.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden)`, which matches the App Store and Apple's sample. Tapping it should open a sheet. HIG has no rule for this, so it is a convention, not a requirement.
- In the transcript, the composer occupies the bottom. Per HIG ("defer to content at the bottom... or there's no bottom toolbar"), in-thread search, if any, should go at the top or be minimized.

### Gaps
- I found no Apple-authored description of the iOS 26 Messages layout (where compose and filters sit). Apple's "All New Features iOS 26" PDF would not parse ([PDF](https://www.apple.com/os/pdf/All_New_Features_iOS_26_Sept_2025.pdf)).
- I found no HIG statement on avatar or account buttons in nav bars.
- I did not fetch the `ToolbarItemPlacement` reference to confirm `.topBarTrailing` / `.bottomBar` semantics on iOS 26. They are used in Apple's WWDC sample code.

## 4. Menus, pull-down buttons and context menus on iOS

### Takeaway
Keep menus short. Put important items first and group them with separators (about three groups at most in context menus). Use one level of submenu, and split a submenu that has more than about five items. Labels are verbs in title case with no articles. Add an ellipsis when the action needs more input. Icons go on the leading edge in iOS 26; give icons to all items in a group or to none. Destructive items go last, render red, and need confirmation. A pull-down menu should have at least three items. Context-menu actions must also exist somewhere in the main interface. System menus draw their items in the system menu font, so SwiftUI `Menu` items do not reliably take custom fonts.

### Cited Findings
- HIG Menus, labels: "write a label that clearly and succinctly describes it," using verbs. "Use title-style capitalization." "Remove articles like a, an, and the." "Append an ellipsis... when the action requires more information before it can complete." "Show people when a menu item is unavailable" (dimmed). — [HIG Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus)
- HIG Menus, icons: "Use menu item icons sparingly and with purpose." Use standard icons for Share, Print and Search. "Don't display an icon if you can't find one that clearly represents the menu item." Provide "icons for all menu items in a group, or none of them." Change log: July 28, 2025 added icon guidance; June 8, 2026 updated it (WWDC26 cycle). — [HIG Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus)
- HIG Menus, organization: "Prefer listing important or frequently used menu items first." Group related items with separators. "Be mindful of menu length." On submenus: "restrict them to a single level"; "If a submenu contains more than about five items, consider creating a new menu"; use a submenu "when a term appears in more than two menu items in the same group." Toggles: use a changeable label (Show Map / Hide Map) or a checkmark. — [HIG Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus)
- HIG Menus, iOS/iPadOS layouts: small (four icon-only items in a top row), medium (three icon-plus-label items in a top row, as in Notes: Scan, Lock, Pin), or large (the default list). API: `UIMenu.preferredElementSize`. — [HIG Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus)
- WWDC25 323: "Icons are consistently on the leading edge" in menus. In SwiftUI, use `Button("Undo", systemImage: ...)` inside `Section`s. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- WWDC25 356: "We recommend populating your menus with symbols where it helps recognition." For closely related actions, "Use the symbol once to introduce the group, and let text do the rest." "When there's no clear shorthand, a text label is always the better choice." — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- HIG Pull-down buttons: "Listing a minimum of three items can help the interaction feel worthwhile." With one or two items, use buttons or toggles instead. "Avoid putting all of a view's actions in one pull-down button." On destructive items: "Let people know when a pull-down button's menu item is destructive, and ask them to confirm their intent." They are shown in red, with an action sheet (iOS) to confirm. "Use SF Symbols." A More (ellipsis) button "can also hinder discoverability." — [HIG Pull-down buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pull-down-buttons)
- HIG Context menus: "Aim for a small number of menu items." A context menu "isn't for providing advanced or rarely used items." Use one level of submenu, and "you don't want more than about three groups." Destructive items go last: "list them at the end of the menu and identify them as destructive" (`UIMenuElement.Attributes.destructive`). "Always make context menu items available in the main interface, too." On iOS: "Provide either a context menu or an edit menu for an item, but not both." Hide irrelevant items rather than dimming them. — [HIG Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus)
- WWDC25 356: action sheets now spring "from the action itself, which serves as the source." WWDC25 323: dialogs "automatically morph out of the buttons that present them." — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/); [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- Fonts in `Menu` items (non-Apple and forum sources): a developer reports that `Menu`, `Picker` and the confirmation dialog "ignore the given Dynamic Type" set by the app. A forum request asks for custom fonts in context menus, noting that AppKit `NSMenuItem` takes an attributed string but SwiftUI has no equivalent. Core Text defines a system "menu item" UI font type. — [Apple Dev Forums 709955](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/709955); [Apple Dev Forums 123449](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/123449); [CTFontUIFontType](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coretext/ctfontuifonttype)

### Inferences
- Treat the system as rendering iOS menu item rows: system font, sizes that follow the system's Dynamic Type, and leading SF Symbols. A custom brand font will reach the `Menu`'s label (the button) when `.font` is applied directly, but it won't reach the items. Design for that, and don't fight it.
- Thread-row context menu: Rename, Pin, Share, a separator, then Delete (destructive, last). Each of these must also be reachable elsewhere, such as swipe actions or the thread's More menu.
- An account menu with fewer than three items should probably be a sheet or direct buttons, not a pull-down.

### Gaps
- No Apple documentation explicitly states that SwiftUI `Menu` item text ignores `.font`. The evidence is from forums and is indirect.

## 5. Sheets on iPhone (iOS 26)

### Takeaway
Use sheets for brief, scoped tasks, never for navigation, and show only one at a time. On iPhone, consider `.medium` for progressive disclosure; skip it for compose-style content that needs full height. Include a grabber when the sheet resizes, support swipe-to-dismiss, and confirm before discarding unsaved changes. Put Cancel on the leading side and Done on the trailing side; never show Cancel, Done and Back together. In iOS 26, partial-height sheets are inset Liquid Glass and become opaque at full height. Remove `presentationBackground`, and morph the sheet from its toolbar button with a zoom transition.

### Cited Findings
- HIG Sheets: "A sheet helps people perform a scoped task that's closely related to their current context." "For complex or prolonged user flows, consider alternatives to sheets" (on iOS, a full-screen modal). "Display only one sheet at a time from the main interface." "Keep sheet interactions brief and occasional." Avoid sheets for app navigation. iOS sheets can be nonmodal (as in Notes formatting). — [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- HIG Sheets, buttons: Cancel/Close "dismisses a sheet without saving any changes"; Done "dismisses a sheet after completing a task." "If you provide a Done button, always pair it with a Cancel button... or a Back button." "Avoid showing all three buttons... together." On iOS: "the Cancel button belongs on the leading edge of the top toolbar. When present, the Done button belongs on the trailing edge." In multi-step sheets, Back replaces Cancel after the first step and Done activates at the end. Change log: March 24, 2026, "Updated guidance for button placement" (post-iOS 26.0). — [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- HIG Sheets, detents: large (full) and medium (about half). "Specifying only medium prevents the sheet from expanding to full height." "In an iPhone app, consider supporting the medium detent to allow progressive disclosure," as share sheets do. Skip medium for compose sheets in Messages and Mail. "Include a grabber in a resizable sheet." "Support swiping to dismiss a sheet," and with unsaved changes "use an action sheet to let them confirm." — [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- The HIG Sheets page itself does not mention Liquid Glass or inset sheets. — [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- WWDC25 323: "On iOS 26, partial height sheets are inset by default with a Liquid Glass background." "At smaller heights, the bottom edges pull in." "When transitioning to a full height sheet, the glass background gradually transitions, becoming opaque and anchoring to the edge of the screen." "If you've used the presentationBackground modifier... consider removing that." To morph from a button, mark the toolbar item with `.matchedTransitionSource(id:in:)` and the sheet content with `.navigationTransition(.zoom(sourceID:in:))`. Detents example: `.presentationDetents([.height(180), .medium, .large])`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- WWDC25 356: when "dragging a sheet upward, Liquid Glass subtly recedes, becoming more opaque." "When a task interrupts the main flow, pair Liquid Glass with a dimming layer." — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)

### Inferences
- A profile or account sheet presented from a top-bar avatar should morph from the avatar (zoom transition), use `[.medium, .large]` with a grabber, and have a single Done or Close button. Settings-style content with no edits needs only Close (the standard X) on the leading side, or Done; Cancel plus Done applies when there are edits.
- A "New thread" flow, if it has options such as runtime or project, fits a sheet. For full composition, consider `.large` only, like Mail and Messages compose, or push directly into an empty thread.
- Never stack a second sheet (for example Settings, then Account) on top of the first. Push within the sheet's own `NavigationStack` instead.

### Gaps
- The HIG page has no iOS 26 sheet visuals; the only source is the WWDC25 sessions. HIG does not specify a grabber size or default-detent rules.

## 6. System app patterns for chat-like apps on iPhone (Messages, Mail)

### Takeaway
Apple's own iOS 26 pattern for a list root screen with compose: a large title, then a bottom Liquid Glass toolbar with filter on the leading side, search in the middle and compose on the trailing side. Account-like or secondary actions go in the top bar. The detail screen keeps Back plus a few trailing items. Only the toolbar layout is primary-sourced (WWDC sample code and the HIG search page). The descriptions of Messages specifically come from secondary sources.

### Cited Findings
- HIG Search fields names Mail and Notes as apps whose bottom-toolbar search sits "alongside other controls". — [HIG Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)
- WWDC26 292 describes Mail's bottom search as "adjacent to other primary actions," with the field "accommodating both a leading, and a trailing action." — [WWDC26 292](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/292/)
- WWDC25 323's Mail-style bottom bar code is FilterPicker, then a flexible spacer, then search, then a fixed spacer, then NewMessageButton. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- HIG Search fields: Mail on iPhone uses a scope bar to switch from the entire mailbox to the current one. Mail and Messages use tokens (contacts, photos). — [HIG Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)
- HIG Sheets: compose sheets in Messages and Mail use full height, without the medium detent. — [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- HIG Toolbars (watchOS section): Mail's New Message is a scrolling toolbar button. This is watchOS-only guidance. — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- Press and user reports say iOS 26 moved search and compose to the bottom in Mail, and Apple's marketing says search sits at the bottom in Messages, Mail, Notes and Music. These are secondary sources. — [Pipedot](https://pipedot.org/6Y1ZG); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1975163/can-you-move-search-bar-to-top-ios-26); [Apple Community thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256137412?page=2)

### Inferences
- Mapping this to the app: the thread list root becomes a large title ("Threads" or the workspace name) with a trailing avatar (account) and an optional More `Menu`. The bottom bar holds an optional filter (leading), search (`.searchable` on the split view) and New thread (trailing compose symbol). The transcript screen has Back, the title, and one or two trailing items. The composer is its bottom chrome, with no separate bottom toolbar. Context menus on thread rows mirror the More-menu actions.

### Gaps
- I couldn't get an Apple-primary description of the iOS 26 Messages toolbar (compose placement, filter menu). The Apple features PDF did not parse.
