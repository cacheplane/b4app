# iOS accessibility guidance and verification for a SwiftUI chat app (ink/white/cobalt palette, icon-only buttons, custom-drawn runtime marks)

Scope: Apple HIG (Accessibility, Color, VoiceOver, Typography), SwiftUI accessibility API reference, Accessibility Inspector docs, XCUIAutomation audit API, WWDC23 10035 and WWDC24 10073, and the local `xcrun simctl ui` help from Xcode 26.6 (build 17F113). Gathered 2026-11-01. HIG pages were read through the `developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/...json` endpoints, which serve the same content as the rendered pages. The rendered-page URL is cited below.

## Contrast minimums, custom colors, Increase Contrast, and not relying on color alone

### Takeaway
Apple's HIG takes WCAG AA as its guidance: 4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt, and 3:1 for text at 18 pt and up or bold text at any size. Every custom color needs light, dark, and increased-contrast variants. If the default palette misses the minimum, the app must at least meet it when Increase Contrast is on. Color must never be the only carrier of state or meaning.

### Cited Findings
- Apple says Accessibility Inspector "uses the following values from WCAG Level AA as guidance". Up to 17 pt, all weights: **4.5:1**. 18 pt, all weights: **3:1**. Bold, all sizes: **3:1**. — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- On what counts as "large": the HIG Accessibility page has no separate definition. The table itself is the definition: 18 pt or larger at any weight, or bold at any size, gets the 3:1 minimum. — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "If your app doesn't provide this minimum contrast by default, ensure it at least provides a higher contrast color scheme when the system setting Increase Contrast is turned on. If your app supports Dark Mode, make sure to check the minimum contrast in both light and dark appearances." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "Prefer system-defined colors. These colors have their own accessible variants that automatically adapt when people adjust their color preferences, such as enabling Increase Contrast or toggling between the light and dark appearances." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "Make sure all your app's colors work well in light, dark, and increased contrast contexts." — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- "If you define a custom color, make sure to supply light and dark variants, and an increased contrast option for each variant that provides a significantly higher amount of visual differentiation. Even if your app ships in a single appearance mode, provide both light and dark colors to support Liquid Glass adaptivity in these contexts." — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- "Make sure your interface maintains sufficient contrast by avoiding overlap of similar colors in the content layer and controls when possible." — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- Liquid Glass (iOS 26): "Apply color sparingly to the Liquid Glass material, and to symbols or text on the material… reserve it for elements that truly benefit from emphasis, such as status indicators or primary actions. To emphasize primary actions, apply color to the background rather than to symbols or text… Refrain from adding color to the background of multiple controls." — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- "Avoid hard-coding system color values in your app… The actual color values may fluctuate from release to release." Also: "Avoid redefining the semantic meanings of dynamic system colors." — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- "Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate between objects, indicate interactivity, or communicate essential information… you can use text labels or glyph shapes to identify objects or states." — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- "Convey information with more than color alone… people who are color blind may have particular difficulty with pairings such as red-green and blue-orange. Offer visual indicators, like distinct shapes or icons, in addition to color." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- SwiftUI exposes `colorSchemeContrast` ("The contrast associated with the color scheme of this environment") and `accessibilityDifferentiateWithoutColor` ("Whether the system preference for Differentiate without Color is enabled") as environment values. — [SwiftUI EnvironmentValues](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues)
- Accessibility Inspector's Differentiate Without Color toggle "replaces UI elements that rely solely on color to convey information". Apple's verification guidance: "Ensure that your app doesn't rely solely on color to differentiate between objects or communicate important information." — [Testing system accessibility features in your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app)
- Accessibility Inspector includes a color contrast calculator: Window > Show Color Contrast Calculator (Option-Command-C). — [Testing system accessibility features in your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app)

### Inferences
- The single cobalt accent needs a 4.5:1 check wherever it colors body-size text or links (17 pt or smaller). That applies on white in light mode and on ink in dark mode. Where it colors icons, bold labels, or 18 pt+ text, 3:1 is the bar. Each color asset should get a High Contrast variant (Xcode color sets support "High Contrast" appearances), plus Any/Dark variants.
- A runtime mark that tells Codex, Claude, and B4 apart only by hue fails "more than color alone". Each mark needs a distinct shape or glyph and/or a text label, and should stay distinguishable under Grayscale and Differentiate Without Color.
- Liquid Glass guidance favors putting the cobalt on the background of one primary action, such as the send button, over tinting many glyphs.

### Gaps
- The HIG doesn't say which point system the 17/18 pt thresholds use against WCAG's own "large text" definition. From memory and not verified this session, WCAG defines large text as 18 pt regular or 14 pt bold, in CSS points. Apple's table instead gives 3:1 to bold text at **all** sizes, which is looser than WCAG for bold text under 14 pt. The report should flag this rather than treat the two as identical.
- No Apple-published contrast ratio for non-text UI components (icons, control borders). The HIG table covers text only. The WCAG 1.4.11 3:1 non-text rule was not found on Apple pages.

## VoiceOver: labels, grouping, traits, decorative content, custom actions, custom-drawn marks

### Takeaway
Give every icon-only control and every meaningful custom-drawn mark a descriptive label. Hide purely decorative imagery. Make each list row one combined element so its sub-buttons become custom actions. Make gesture-only interactions, including swipe actions and hover overlays, reachable as accessibility actions.

### Cited Findings
- "Provide alternative labels for all key interface elements… System-provided controls have generic labels by default, but you should provide more descriptive labels that convey your app's functionality. Add labels to any custom elements your app defines. Be sure to keep your descriptions up-to-date as your app's interface and content change." — [HIG VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)
- "Exclude purely decorative images from VoiceOver… Excluding these images shows respect for people's time and reduces cognitive load." The page points developers to `accessibilityHidden(_:)`, `accessibilityElement`, and `isAccessibilityElement`. — [HIG VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)
- "Describe meaningful images… describe only the information the image itself conveys." — [HIG VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)
- "Specify how elements are grouped, ordered, or linked… Examine your app for places where relationships among elements are visual only. Then, describe these relationships to VoiceOver." — [HIG VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)
- "Use titles and headings to help people navigate your information hierarchy." Also: "Support the VoiceOver rotor when possible" (`AccessibilityRotorEntry` in SwiftUI). — [HIG VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)
- "Inform VoiceOver when visible content or layout changes occur" (`AccessibilityNotification`). This bears on streaming assistant output. — [HIG VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)
- `Image(decorative:bundle:)` "Creates an unlabeled, decorative image." Also: "SwiftUI ignores this image for accessibility purposes." — [Image.init(decorative:bundle:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/image/init(decorative:bundle:))
- `AccessibilityChildBehavior` cases. `.combine`: "Any child accessibility element's properties are merged into the new accessibility element." `.contain`: "Any child accessibility elements become children of the new accessibility element." `.ignore`: "Any child accessibility elements become hidden." — [AccessibilityChildBehavior](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/accessibilitychildbehavior)
- With `.accessibilityElement(children: .combine)` on a message row (unread indicator, contents, favorite and reply buttons), labels are combined, important traits merged, and the row's buttons become custom actions: "the attributes and actions of multiple elements are combined together." — [WWDC24 Catch up on accessibility in SwiftUI](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/)
- A shape-based indicator (`Circle()`) gets an element by adding `.accessibilityLabel("Unread")`: "The accessibility label modifier will first create an element for my indicator view since it doesn't have one by default." SwiftUI hides it from accessibility automatically when its opacity is 0. — [WWDC24 10073](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/)
- SF Symbols: "Many [symbols] provide default labels when a meaning can be inferred, like a star or a reply symbol. Others need an explicit label… and fallback to their name as the label." — [WWDC24 10073](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/)
- iOS 18 added an `isEnabled:` parameter to accessibility modifiers, for example `.accessibilityLabel("Super Favorite", isEnabled: isSuperFavorite)`. The page also documents `accessibilityHidden(_:isEnabled:)`. — [WWDC24 10073](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/); [SwiftUI Accessibility modifiers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view-accessibility)
- A label closure that appends to SwiftUI's generated label (`.accessibilityLabel { label in Text(rating); label }`) exists because "A regular label modifier would wipe out the great descriptive contents that SwiftUI already provides to the element." — [WWDC24 10073](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/)
- `.accessibilityActions { … }` "extracts controls and transforms them into custom actions", so VoiceOver can perform hover or overlay controls "without having to present the overlay." — [WWDC24 10073](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/)
- Other modifiers in Apple's collection: `accessibilityHint(_:)` ("Communicates to the user what happens after performing the view's action"), `accessibilityValue(_:)`, `accessibilityAddTraits(_:)`/`accessibilityRemoveTraits(_:)`, `accessibilityAction(named:_:)`, `accessibilityActions(category:_:)`, `accessibilityAdjustableAction(_:)`, `accessibilityRepresentation(representation:)` ("Replaces one or more accessibility elements for this view with new accessibility elements"), `accessibilityChildren(children:)` (synthetic children, useful for Canvas-drawn content), `accessibilitySortPriority(_:)`, `accessibilityInputLabels(_:)` (Voice Control names), and `accessibilityShowsLargeContentViewer()`. — [SwiftUI Accessibility modifiers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view-accessibility)
- "Use the styling system over creating a custom view whenever possible otherwise, you'll have to re-apply the same accessibility properties you could be getting automatically." — [WWDC24 10073](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10073/)
- Use accessibility *identifiers*, not labels, for UI-test lookups. A label like `QUOTE_TEXTVIEW` was flagged because VoiceOver reads it aloud. — [WWDC23 Perform accessibility audits for your app](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10035/)
- For Voice Control, "label interface elements appropriately." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

### Inferences
- Icon-only buttons such as send, stop, attach, and the sidebar profile menu should be `Button { } label: { Label("Send", systemImage: …) }` with `.labelStyle(.iconOnly)`, or carry an explicit `.accessibilityLabel`. A `Label` keeps the title for VoiceOver and Voice Control. A bare `Image(systemName:)` falls back to the symbol name when no meaning can be inferred.
- A conversation row (runtime mark, title, preview, timestamp, unread dot) should use `.accessibilityElement(children: .combine)`. Its custom-drawn runtime mark should have `.accessibilityLabel("Claude Code")`, or equivalent, so the runtime is spoken. If the title already names the runtime, hide the mark with `.accessibilityHidden(true)` to avoid repeating it.
- If a mark is drawn in `Canvas` or `Shape`, it has no element by default (WWDC24). Adding `.accessibilityLabel` creates one, and `.accessibilityChildren`/`.accessibilityRepresentation` cover more complex drawings.
- For streaming assistant replies, post an `AccessibilityNotification.Announcement` when a turn completes, rather than on every token. This follows the "inform VoiceOver when visible content… changes" guidance and is not stated explicitly by Apple.

### Gaps
- Apple's HIG VoiceOver page gives no label style rules, such as "don't include the control type, 'button', in the label" or capitalization. The 2019 WWDC session "Writing Great Accessibility Labels" (cited on the HIG page) covers this but was not fetched.
- The `swipeActions(edge:allowsFullSwipe:content:)` reference says nothing about VoiceOver ([swipeActions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/swipeactions(edge:allowsfullswipe:content:))). The common belief is that SwiftUI exposes swipe actions to VoiceOver as row actions automatically. This was not confirmed in Apple docs this session and should be verified on device. The safe pattern is to also expose the same actions through `.accessibilityAction(named:)` or a context menu.
- No Apple guidance found on trait usage specifics (for example, when to add `.isHeader` or `.isButton` to custom rows) beyond what SwiftUI applies automatically.

## Mobility: hit targets, spacing, alternatives to gestures

### Takeaway
On iOS, 44x44 pt is the default control size and 28x28 pt the absolute minimum. Leave about 12 pt of padding around bezeled controls and about 24 pt around unbezeled ones. Every gesture needs an onscreen alternative.

### Cited Findings
- Control sizes for iOS and iPadOS: **default 44x44 pt, minimum 28x28 pt**. Other platforms: macOS 28x28/20x20, visionOS 60x60/28x28, watchOS 44x44/28x28, tvOS 66x66/56x56. — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "Consider spacing between controls as important as size… add about 12 points of padding around elements that include a bezel. For elements without a bezel, about 24 points of padding works well around the element's visible edges." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "Offer alternatives to gestures… if you use a swipe gesture to dismiss a view, also make a button available so people can tap or use an assistive device." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "Support simple gestures for common interactions… avoid custom multifinger and multihand gestures." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Accessibility Inspector's Hit region audit "checks whether the size of an element is too small for a person to interact with." — [Performing accessibility audits for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-audits-for-your-app)
- Developer report (unofficial): a SwiftUI `Button` whose label is only an `Image` gets a smaller tap target, the image bounds, than a `Label`-based or convenience-initializer button. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 849832](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/849832) (forum post, not Apple documentation)
- Developer report (unofficial): Accessibility Inspector flags a SwiftUI `TextField` hit area as too small, and `frame(minHeight:)` doesn't enlarge it. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 703257](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/703257)

### Inferences
- Small icon-only buttons drawn at around 20-24 pt glyph size should get `.frame(minWidth: 44, minHeight: 44)` plus `.contentShape(Rectangle())`, so the hit region reaches 44x44 without changing the visual. The audit's `hitRegion` check then verifies this.
- Swipe-to-archive or delete on conversation rows needs a non-gesture path, such as a context menu or an edit mode button, per "Offer alternatives to gestures".

### Gaps
- The exact text of the Inspector's hit-region issue (the brief's "Hit region is too small", versus something like "Hit area is too small") was not confirmed in Apple docs. Apple only describes the category. The threshold the audit uses (44 pt or something else) is not documented.

## Verification tooling: Accessibility Inspector, performAccessibilityAudit, Simulator settings from the command line

### Takeaway
Accessibility Inspector audits one screen at a time, covering element description, hit region, contrast, element detection, clipped text, traits, and Dynamic Type on iOS. `XCUIApplication.performAccessibilityAudit(for:_:)` (iOS 17+) runs the same audits in UI tests and fails the test automatically. `xcrun simctl ui <device> content_size|increase_contrast|appearance` sets Dynamic Type, Increase Contrast, and Dark Mode from the CLI.

### Cited Findings
**Accessibility Inspector**
- Open via Xcode > Open Developer Tool > Accessibility Inspector. It audits for "clipped text and unlabeled elements, and uses appropriate text size and color contrast levels." — [Accessibility Inspector](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/accessibility-inspector)
- Workflow: pick the app from the target menu, use Options to choose audit types, then "Run Audit… for the current screen". Results are numbered per run. "make sure to run an audit for each of the screens in your app." — [Performing accessibility audits for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-audits-for-your-app)
- Audit categories for all platforms: **Element description** ("All accessible elements must provide some context-specific, descriptive label"), **Hit region**, **Contrast** ("sufficient color contrast between overlapping elements"), and **Element detection** ("elements contain inaccessible content that your app might need to expose as separate accessibility children"). For iOS, watchOS, and tvOS: **Clipped text** ("whether clipping might occur for any text labels at larger Dynamic Type sizes"), **Traits**, and **Dynamic Type** ("whether the text in your app supports Dynamic Type"). For macOS: **Parent/child** and **Action**. — [Performing accessibility audits for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-audits-for-your-app)
- Issue tools: the dropdown shows a detailed explanation, a click highlights the element, a double-click inspects it, the Capture icon takes a screenshot with a highlight, "Fix suggestions" offers guidance on colors, font sizes, or APIs, and File > Save Audit Report As… exports HTML. — [Performing accessibility audits for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-audits-for-your-app)
- "eliminating all audit issues that Accessibility Inspector reports doesn't guarantee a fully accessible app. Always test your app with various assistive apps, such as VoiceOver." — [Performing accessibility audits for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-audits-for-your-app)
- Example issue text from the WWDC demo: "Element has no description" on an image view. — [WWDC23 10035](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10035/)
- Settings pane toggles, which update the target device's system settings: Invert colors, Increase contrast, Reduce transparency, Grayscale (iOS), Reduce motion, Full Keyboard Access, Differentiate Without Color, Button shapes (iOS), On/Off labels (iOS), Bold fonts (iOS), and Dynamic type (font size). — [Testing system accessibility features in your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app)

**XCUIApplication.performAccessibilityAudit**
- Declaration: `@MainActor func performAccessibilityAudit(for auditTypes: XCUIAccessibilityAuditType = .all, _ issueHandler: ((XCUIAccessibilityAuditIssue) throws -> Bool)? = nil) throws`. Availability: iOS 17.0+, macOS 14.0+, tvOS 17.0+, watchOS 10.0+. Apple's page now lives under the XCUIAutomation framework and lists "Xcode 16.3+". — [performAccessibilityAudit(for:_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcuiautomation/xcuiapplication/performaccessibilityaudit(for:_:))
- `XCUIAccessibilityAuditType` is an `OptionSet` with members `.action`, `.all`, `.contrast`, `.dynamicType`, `.elementDetection`, `.hitRegion`, `.parentChild`, `.sufficientElementDescription`, `.textClipped`, and `.trait`. — [XCUIAccessibilityAuditType](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcuiautomation/xcuiaccessibilityaudittype)
- "Calling performAccessibilityAudit on your XCUIApplication will audit the current view for accessibility issues just as the Inspector does." "There's no need for assertions: if any issues are found, your test automatically fails." "An audit is limited to the elements on the screen." "Audits shouldn't substitute real testing with assistive technologies." — [WWDC23 10035](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10035/)
- Issue handler: returning `true` **ignores** the issue, and returning `false` reports it as a failure. The handler can filter on `issue.auditType`, `issue.element?.label`, the element type, or the identifier. The demo sets `continueAfterFailure = true` to collect every issue. Failures appear inline in the editor and in Report navigator > Tests, with element screenshots. — [WWDC23 10035](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10035/)
- In UIKit, `automationElements` can keep decorative views visible to UI tests while `accessibilityElements` hides them from assistive technologies. — [WWDC23 10035](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10035/)

**Simulator from the command line** (verbatim from `xcrun simctl ui` help, Xcode 26.6 build 17F113, run locally 2026-11-01; no URL)
- Usage: `simctl ui <device> <option> [<arguments>]`.
- `content_size` with no argument prints the current category. Valid values: standard sizes `extra-small, small, medium, large, extra-large, extra-extra-large, extra-extra-extra-large`, and extended range sizes `accessibility-medium, accessibility-large, accessibility-extra-large, accessibility-extra-extra-large, accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large`. Also `increment` and `decrement`. Example: `xcrun simctl ui booted content_size accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large`.
- `increase_contrast [enabled | disabled]` toggles Increase Contrast.
- `appearance [light | dark]` sets the appearance.
- The `simctl ui` help in Xcode 26.6 lists no options for Bold Text, Reduce Transparency, Reduce Motion, or Button Shapes. Those come from Accessibility Inspector's Settings pane, Xcode's Environment Overrides, or the Simulator's Settings app.

**SwiftUI environment values to read for adaptive behavior**
- `accessibilityReduceMotion`, `accessibilityReduceTransparency`, `accessibilityDifferentiateWithoutColor`, `accessibilityInvertColors`, `accessibilityVoiceOverEnabled`, `legibilityWeight` ("The font weight to apply to text", which reflects Bold Text), `colorSchemeContrast`, `dynamicTypeSize`, and `accessibilityLargeContentViewerEnabled`. — [SwiftUI EnvironmentValues](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues)
- `accessibilityShowButtonShapes` ("Whether the system preference for Show Button Shapes is enabled"; iOS 14+) is **deprecated and renamed to `accessibilityShowBorders`**. "If this property's value is true, interactive custom controls such as buttons should be drawn in such a way that their edges and borders are clearly visible." — [accessibilityShowButtonShapes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityshowbuttonshapes)

### Inferences
- A practical CI gate is one UI test per primary screen (conversation list, conversation detail, composer, settings) that calls `try app.performAccessibilityAudit()`. Run it again after `simctl ui booted content_size accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large`, and again in dark mode with `increase_contrast enabled`, to catch clipping and contrast regressions under the hardest settings. Known false positives should be filtered in the issue handler by identifier, not label.
- Custom borderless icon buttons should draw a visible border or background when `accessibilityShowBorders` (formerly `accessibilityShowButtonShapes`) is true. System button styles handle this automatically.

### Gaps
- The `performAccessibilityAudit` reference page itself has no discussion text. The handler semantics come from WWDC23. The "Xcode 16.3+" label conflicts with the API's WWDC23 (Xcode 15) debut. It most likely reflects the docs moving to the XCUIAutomation framework, which is unverified.
- The availability version of `accessibilityShowBorders` and the iOS release that renamed it were not fetched.
- Xcode's Environment Overrides panel (debug bar), and exactly which settings it exposes in Xcode 26, was not confirmed from Apple docs this session.
- No `simctl` command was found for Bold Text, Reduce Motion, or Reduce Transparency. `defaults write` hacks exist in community posts but were not researched.

## Large accessibility sizes: expected layout changes; Bold Text, Reduce Transparency, Reduce Motion

### Takeaway
At AX sizes (body text 28-53 pt), Apple expects the layout to reflow rather than truncate. Stack text above secondary items such as glyphs and timestamps, cut columns, let labels wrap to as many lines as needed, scale meaningful icons, and keep the hierarchy stable. The app should also honor Bold Text (custom fonts must do this themselves), Reduce Transparency, and Reduce Motion (swap motion for fades).

### Cited Findings
- "Make sure people can adjust the size of your text or icons… Ideally, give people the option to enlarge text by at least 200 percent." — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- iOS default text size is 17 pt, with an 11 pt minimum. "If you're using a custom font with a thin weight, aim for larger than the recommended sizes." — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- iOS Body style: Large (default) is 17/22 pt. AX1 28/34, AX2 33/40, AX3 40/48, AX4 47/56, AX5 53/62 (size/leading; emphasized weight Semibold). — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Make sure your app's layout adapts to all font sizes." Test with Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Larger Text > Larger Accessibility Text Sizes. — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Increase the size of meaningful interface icons as font size increases… When you use SF Symbols, you get icons that scale automatically with Dynamic Type size changes." — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Keep text truncation to a minimum as font size increases… aim to display as much useful text at the largest accessibility font size as you do at the largest standard font size. Avoid truncating text in scrollable regions unless people can open a separate view to read the rest… configuring it to use as many lines as needed." — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Consider adjusting your layout at large font sizes… inline items (like glyphs and timestamps) and container boundaries can crowd text… consider using a stacked layout where text appears above secondary items… Reduce the number of columns when the font size increases." — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Maintain a consistent information hierarchy regardless of the current font size… keep primary elements toward the top of a view." — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Bold Text: "System fonts automatically support Dynamic Type… and respond when people turn on accessibility features, such as Bold Text. If you use a custom font, make sure it implements the same behaviors." — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Accessibility Inspector's Bold fonts and Dynamic type guidance: "Ensure that your app doesn't make assumptions about font size, and your UI scales appropriately for different font weights and sizes." — [Testing system accessibility features in your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app)
- Reduce Transparency "improves contrast and legibility by reducing transparency and blur effects on certain backgrounds." Increase Contrast "raises color contrast between app foreground and background colors." — [Testing system accessibility features in your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app)
- Reduce Motion: "ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion." Best practices: tighten springs to reduce bounce, track animations directly with gestures, avoid animating z-axis depth, replace x/y/z transitions with fades, and avoid animating into and out of blurs. — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Button Shapes "draws a more prominent UI for buttons to help differentiate them from noninteractive elements." — [Testing system accessibility features in your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app); see the `accessibilityShowButtonShapes` → `accessibilityShowBorders` rename above ([docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityshowbuttonshapes)).
- `accessibilityShowsLargeContentViewer()` adds a large content view for the Large Content Viewer, the iOS feature that shows an enlarged version of small, fixed-size controls on long press at AX sizes. — [SwiftUI Accessibility modifiers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view-accessibility)

### Inferences
- Conversation rows: at `dynamicTypeSize.isAccessibilitySize`, switch the `HStack` (runtime mark, title, timestamp) to a `VStack`, or use `ViewThatFits`/`AnyLayout`. Let the title wrap with `lineLimit(nil)` or a higher limit. Scale the runtime mark with `@ScaledMetric` so it grows with text, since meaningful icons should grow.
- Icon-only toolbar and composer buttons that can't grow, such as a navigation-bar or Liquid Glass toolbar, should adopt `accessibilityShowsLargeContentViewer()`.
- Custom fonts, if any, need to map Bold Text through `legibilityWeight == .bold`. System `Font.TextStyle` handles this automatically.
- Ink-on-white surfaces using `.ultraThinMaterial` or Liquid Glass should fall back to opaque fills when `accessibilityReduceTransparency` is true. Streaming or typing animations should become fades or nothing when `accessibilityReduceMotion` is true.

### Gaps
- The HIG doesn't say outright that everything must scroll at AX sizes. Scrolling is implied only by the truncation guidance about scrollable regions.
- The Large Content Viewer behavior described above comes from general knowledge, not a document fetched this session. Only the SwiftUI modifier's abstract was confirmed.
- `ViewThatFits`, `AnyLayout`, `@ScaledMetric`, and `DynamicTypeSize.isAccessibilitySize` are named from SwiftUI knowledge. Their reference pages were not fetched this session.
