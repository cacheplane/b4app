# Managed PostgreSQL offerings for the b4 hub (late 2026)

Scope: a sourced comparison of managed PostgreSQL providers for a small multi-tenant Rust service (sqlx, PostgreSQL 17 features, forced row-level security on tenant tables, an owner role used only by migrations and an application role `b4hub_app` that migrations create and grant to, a JSONB change log with time-based pruning, planned LISTEN/NOTIFY for cross-instance signalling, 25 s long-poll readers). Two environments (prod and test), each with its own database. Google Cloud and Cloudflare excluded by the owner; AWS allowed; independent vendors considered.

All pages were read on 2026-11-01 unless a different date is given. Anything marked "third-party" came from an aggregator or a blog, not the vendor, and should be re-checked against the vendor page before it is relied on. Prices are USD list prices with no committed-use discount.

Providers covered: Neon, Supabase (Postgres only), Crunchy Bridge, AWS RDS for PostgreSQL, AWS Aurora PostgreSQL (Serverless v2 and Global Database), Fly.io Managed Postgres (MPG), Render Postgres, Railway Postgres, DigitalOcean Managed Databases, Tiger Cloud (formerly Timescale), Prisma Postgres, Xata.

## Key question 1: PostgreSQL 17/18 availability and extension support (uuid-ossp, pgcrypto, pg_stat_statements)

### Takeaway
Every candidate offers PostgreSQL 17; most also offer 18 (Neon, RDS, Aurora, Render, DigitalOcean, Crunchy Bridge). Fly.io MPG stops at 17 and cannot do in-place major upgrades; Prisma Postgres is pinned to 17; Supabase requires an offline 15-to-17 upgrade for older projects. The three extensions are standard contrib modules and are confirmed on DigitalOcean and implied elsewhere, but only DigitalOcean's docs were checked extension by extension.

### Cited Findings
- Neon: "Neon currently supports Postgres 14, 15, 16, 17, and 18"; each major is supported five years from release — [Neon Postgres version policy](https://neon.com/docs/postgresql/postgres-version-policy)
- Neon's `neon_superuser` role is granted `pg_maintain` on "Postgres 17+" and `pg_signal_autovacuum_worker` on "Postgres 18+", and has EXECUTE on `pg_stat_statements_reset()`, which implies pg_stat_statements is available — [Neon roles](https://neon.com/docs/manage/roles)
- Supabase: upgrading from Postgres 15 to 17 requires disabling the deprecated extensions `plcoffee`, `plls`, `plv8`, `timescaledb`, and `pgjwt`; the project goes offline during the upgrade; "Postgres 15 remains supported until its end of life on Supabase" — [Supabase upgrading](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/upgrading)
- Crunchy Bridge pricing page: Hobby-0 and Hobby-1 support "PG15–18 only"; Hobby-2, Hobby-4, Standard, and Memory-optimized support "PG14–18" — [Crunchy Bridge plans and pricing](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/plans-pricing); the marketing product page still says "Deploy on Postgres 15, 16, or 17" — [Crunchy Bridge product page](https://www.crunchydata.com/products/crunchy-bridge) (the two pages conflict; the docs page is more specific and more recent)
- RDS for PostgreSQL 18 became available on 2025-11-14, starting with 18.1 — [AWS What's New, 2025-11-14](https://aws.amazon.com/ko/about-aws/whats-new/2025/11/amazon-rds-postgresql-major-version-18/) (Korean-language copy of the announcement; the English page was not in results); the release notes list 18.4 and 17.10 among the May 2026 minors — [RDS for PostgreSQL release notes](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/PostgreSQLReleaseNotes/postgresql-versions.html)
- RDS `rds_superuser` can "Add extensions that are available for use with Amazon RDS" — [rds_superuser](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Appendix.PostgreSQL.CommonDBATasks.Roles.rds_superuser.html)
- Aurora PostgreSQL release notes list "Aurora PostgreSQL 18.6.1, October 15, 2026" and "Aurora PostgreSQL 17.11.1, October 15, 2026" — [Aurora PostgreSQL releases](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraPostgreSQLReleaseNotes/AuroraPostgreSQL.Updates.html)
- Aurora Serverless v2 0 to 384 ACU range requires "14.23 and higher, 15.18 and higher, 16.14 and higher, 17.10 and higher, 18.4 and higher" — [Aurora serverless how it works](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2.how-it-works.html)
- Fly.io MPG: "You can choose Postgres 16 or 17 when creating a cluster"; "In-place major version upgrades are not supported. You must create a new cluster and migrate with pg_dump and pg_restore"; pgvector included, PostGIS optional at provisioning; other extensions by support request — [Fly.io MPG overview](https://docs.fly.io/mpg/overview)
- Render: "Major versions 13 through 18 are available for all new instances"; the CLI "Defaults to the latest supported version (18)" — [Render Postgres creating and connecting](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-creating-connecting)
- Railway: the official image is `ghcr.io/railwayapp-templates/postgres-ssl` or `postgres-ha/postgres-patroni`, selected by a major-version tag such as `:16`; "Minor version tags, such as :16.10, aren't supported" — [Railway CLI postgres](https://docs.railway.com/cli/postgres); the Postgres guide says "we do not plan to add extensions to the PostgreSQL templates covered in this guide" and offers separate TimescaleDB, PostGIS, and pgvector templates — [Railway PostgreSQL guide](https://docs.railway.com/guides/postgresql)
- DigitalOcean Standard Edition "supports PostgreSQL v15, v16, v17, and v18 for new clusters" with v18 the default; Advanced Edition supports v16, v17, and v18 — [DigitalOcean PostgreSQL limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/)
- DigitalOcean lists `pgcrypto`, `uuid-ossp`, and `pg_stat_statements` for PG 14 to 18 on Standard Edition and as "Available" or "Pre-installed" (pg_stat_statements) on Advanced Edition — [DigitalOcean supported extensions](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/supported-extensions/)
- Tiger Cloud: minor PostgreSQL versions are applied automatically in the maintenance window; major upgrades are manual, "can take up to 20 minutes" of downtime, and "services with replicas cannot be upgraded" until the replica is deleted — [Tiger Cloud maintenance and upgrades](https://www.tigerdata.com/docs/deploy/tiger-cloud/tiger-cloud-aws/upgrades); TimescaleDB drops PostgreSQL 15 in 2.29.0 (June 2026) — [TimescaleDB upgrade-pg](https://www.tigerdata.com/docs/self-hosted/latest/upgrades/upgrade-pg)
- Prisma Postgres is "managed PostgreSQL 17 with PgBouncer pooling on every plan" — [Prisma Postgres overview](https://www.prisma.io/docs/postgres/introduction/overview)

### Inferences
- For a service that uses PostgreSQL 17 features today, every candidate qualifies; only Fly.io (no in-place major upgrade) and Prisma (single pinned version) constrain the upgrade path to 18 and beyond.
- uuid-ossp, pgcrypto, and pg_stat_statements are core contrib modules shipped with every PostgreSQL build; a provider would have to remove them deliberately. Only DigitalOcean was verified module-by-module; the others should be checked in a trial project before committing.

### Gaps
- No vendor page listing exact extension availability was read for Neon, Supabase, Crunchy Bridge, RDS, Aurora, Fly.io, Render, Railway, Tiger Cloud, Prisma, or Xata; the Neon roles page only implies pg_stat_statements.
- Supabase's default Postgres major for new projects (15 or 17) was not found on the upgrading or pricing pages.
- Tiger Cloud's supported major versions for new services were not found; only the TimescaleDB compatibility policy was located.
- Xata Cloud's Postgres version is not stated on its pricing page — [Xata pricing](https://xata.io/pricing).

## Key question 2: Roles, CREATE ROLE / ALTER ROLE, superuser restrictions, RLS, set_config

### Takeaway
No candidate hands out a real SUPERUSER except Crunchy Bridge (per its marketplace listings; the official docs page could not be reached). Neon, RDS/Aurora, Supabase, Tiger Cloud, and DigitalOcean give an admin role with CREATEROLE and CREATEDB, which is enough for a migration role to create and grant to `b4hub_app`; FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY and per-transaction `set_config(..., true)` are ordinary table-owner and session operations that none of these restrictions touch. Fly.io MPG is the outlier: it exposes only `schema_admin`, `writer`, and `reader` roles.

### Cited Findings
- Neon: `neon_superuser` has `CREATEDB`, `CREATEROLE` ("Provides the ability to create new roles," which also means it can alter and drop roles), `BYPASSRLS` (projects created after 2023-08-15), `REPLICATION`, and membership in `pg_read_all_data`, `pg_write_all_data`, `pg_monitor`, `pg_signal_backend`, `pg_create_subscription`, `pg_maintain` (PG17+); it is `NOLOGIN`; "you can't connect using the Postgres superuser account"; roles created with SQL "are not granted neon_superuser membership" — [Neon roles](https://neon.com/docs/manage/roles)
- RDS: the master user is `CREATE ROLE postgres WITH LOGIN NOSUPERUSER INHERIT CREATEDB CREATEROLE NOREPLICATION VALID UNTIL 'infinity'` and a member of `rds_superuser`, which can "Create roles for users and grant privileges to users", "Grant rds_superuser privileges to user roles", and terminate backends; "you can't connect using the PostgreSQL superuser account" — [rds_superuser](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Appendix.PostgreSQL.CommonDBATasks.Roles.rds_superuser.html)
- Supabase: the `postgres` role is "The default Postgres role. This has admin privileges"; custom roles are created with `create role "role_name" with login password '...'` — [Supabase roles](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/roles); a Supabase GitHub discussion states that on hosted Supabase `postgres` has CREATEROLE and CREATEDB and is not a superuser, the only superuser being the internal `supabase_admin` — [supabase/supabase discussion #50379](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/discussions/50379) (community source); Supabase's `supautils` extension reserves `supabase_admin,supabase_auth_admin,supabase_storage_admin` so CREATEROLE users cannot drop or alter them — [supautils](https://www.github.com/supabase/supautils)
- Crunchy Bridge AWS Marketplace and Azure Marketplace listings describe "superuser access right out of the box" and "superuser-level access enabling management of roles, schemas, permissions" — [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-orfodimax3czu), [Azure Marketplace listing](https://azuremarketplace.microsoft.com/en-us/marketplace/apps/crunchydatasolutionsinc1648056888353.bridge-prod)
- DigitalOcean: "Users cannot access the superuser role to maintain cluster stability"; the default database and user cannot be deleted; Standard Edition extension notes say "If required, assign superuser privileges to enable their use" (wording on the extensions page, which contradicts the limits page) — [DigitalOcean limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/), [DigitalOcean extensions](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/supported-extensions/)
- Tiger Cloud: `tsdbadmin` "has full permissions to modify data in a service" but "Tiger Cloud does not provide superuser access"; `tsdbadmin` "can create other roles or assign individual permissions" with standard PostgreSQL commands — [Tiger Cloud read-only role](https://www.tigerdata.com/docs/deploy/tiger-cloud/tiger-cloud-aws/security/read-only-role)
- Fly.io MPG: `fly mpg users set-role` offers `schema_admin`, `writer`, and `reader` — [fly mpg users set-role](https://fly.io/docs/flyctl/mpg-users-set-role/); a Fly staffer said granting superuser to all users "isn't something they're planning at this time" — [Fly community: MPG superuser permissions](https://community.fly.io/t/mpg-superuser-permissions-for-extensions/28124)
- Railway runs the stock Docker `postgres` image ("uses the official Postgres image from Docker Hub as its base"), where the configured user is the cluster superuser — [Railway PostgreSQL guide](https://docs.railway.com/guides/postgresql)
- PostgreSQL 17 rule: "You must yourself be a superuser to create a new superuser" and "You must be a superuser to create a new role having the BYPASSRLS attribute" — [CREATE ROLE, PostgreSQL 17](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-createrole.html)

### Inferences
- The b4 hub pattern (owner role runs migrations, creates `b4hub_app`, grants to it) works on Neon, RDS, Aurora, Supabase, Tiger Cloud, DigitalOcean, Crunchy Bridge, and Railway because each gives a CREATEROLE-capable admin. `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` is a table-owner operation and `set_config(name, value, true)` is session-local; neither needs superuser.
- `b4hub_app` must not be created with BYPASSRLS; on providers without superuser (everything but Crunchy/Railway) that is impossible anyway, which is the desired outcome. On Neon the admin login inherits BYPASSRLS, so migrations must never be run as the application role and tests of RLS must connect as `b4hub_app`.
- Fly.io MPG's fixed role set (`schema_admin`, `writer`, `reader`) is the one that may not fit a migration-creates-app-role flow; verify in a trial whether `schema_admin` has CREATEROLE.

### Gaps
- Crunchy Bridge's official roles documentation returned 404 at the URL tried (`docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/users-roles`); superuser availability rests on marketplace listings.
- Whether Supabase's `postgres` role carries BYPASSRLS is asserted by a third-party article and not confirmed by Supabase docs.
- Prisma Postgres and Xata do not document the privileges of the user account.
- No provider documents a restriction on `set_config`, `row_security`, or `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`; absence of a documented restriction is not the same as a tested confirmation.

## Key question 3: Connection pooling (mode, LISTEN/NOTIFY, SET LOCAL, prepared statements) and direct connection limits

### Takeaway
Every built-in pooler defaults to transaction mode, which breaks LISTEN/NOTIFY and session-level SET but preserves `SET LOCAL`/`set_config(..., true)` inside a transaction. Protocol-level prepared statements (what sqlx uses) work through PgBouncer 1.21+/1.22+ poolers (Neon, Supabase dedicated pooler, Crunchy, Fly, Railway, DigitalOcean) but not through Supabase's shared Supavisor in transaction mode, and they cause session pinning on RDS Proxy. A LISTEN/NOTIFY worker therefore needs a direct (non-pooled) connection on every provider; small direct-connection limits (DigitalOcean 22 on 1 GiB, Supabase 60 on Micro, Neon 104 on 0.25 CU) are the constraint for a 25 s long-poll design.

### Cited Findings
- Neon: "Neon uses PgBouncer in transaction mode"; `max_client_conn=10000`; `default_pool_size=0.9 * max_connections`; direct `max_connections` by compute size: 0.25 CU 104, 0.5 CU 209, 1 CU 419, 2 CU 839, 4 CU 1678, capped at 4000; unsupported through the pooler: LISTEN/NOTIFY, SET/RESET, WITH HOLD cursors, SQL-level PREPARE/DEALLOCATE, temp tables with PRESERVE/DELETE ROWS, LOAD, session-level advisory locks; protocol-level prepared statements supported "(as of PgBouncer 1.22.0)" with `max_prepared_statements=1000` — [Neon connection pooling](https://neon.com/docs/connect/connection-pooling)
- Supabase: port 5432 is the direct connection and the shared pooler in session mode; port 6543 is transaction mode; "The shared pooler doesn't support prepared statements in transaction mode"; transaction mode also excludes `set`/`reset`, session advisory locks, `listen`/`notify`, temp tables; a dedicated PgBouncer is provisioned "On the Pro Plan and above", transaction mode only, prepared statements on PgBouncer 1.21+; direct connections are IPv6 unless the IPv4 add-on is bought; the shared pooler is IPv4-reachable on every plan — [Supabase connecting to Postgres](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/connecting-to-postgres)
- Supabase direct/pooler connection limits: Nano 60/200, Micro 60/200, Small 90/400, Medium 120/600, Large 160/800 — [Supabase compute and disk](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk)
- RDS Proxy: "By default, RDS Proxy can reuse a connection after each transaction"; PostgreSQL pinning triggers include `SET` commands, `PREPARE`/`DISCARD`/`DEALLOCATE`/`EXECUTE`, temp objects, cursors, "Listening on a notification channel", session advisory locks (`pg_advisory_lock`), and "using SET and set_config commands to assign default values to session variables"; "RDS Proxy does not pin on transaction level advisory locks"; "for PostgreSQL setting a variable leads to session pinning"; "RDS Proxy does not pin connections when you use SET LOCAL" appears only in the MySQL/MariaDB section — [RDS Proxy pinning](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/rds-proxy-pinning.html), [RDS Proxy concepts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/rds-proxy.howitworks.html)
- RDS Proxy keeps a connection to each instance, so "any Aurora serverless instances in such a cluster won't automatically pause" — [Aurora auto-pause](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2-auto-pause.html)
- Crunchy Bridge pricing is "Fully inclusive for all machine costs, hosting, backups, and connection pooling" — [Crunchy pricing](https://www.crunchydata.com/pricing); a third-party listing says Standard tiers include PgBouncer — [FitGap Crunchy Bridge](https://us.fitgap.com/products/015701/crunchy-bridge)
- Fly.io MPG: "Every plan includes high availability, automated backups and connection pooling"; "PgBouncer is included, and its version is pinned" — [Fly pricing](https://fly.io/pricing), [Fly MPG overview](https://docs.fly.io/mpg/overview)
- Render: connection limits by plan memory: under 8 GB 100; 8 to under 16 GB 200; 16 to under 32 GB 400; 32 GB or more 500; legacy 97/197/397 — [Render creating and connecting](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-creating-connecting)
- Railway: `railway postgres pgbouncer add --pool-mode <MODE>` accepts `transaction`, `session`, or `statement`, "Defaults to transaction for add" — [Railway CLI postgres](https://docs.railway.com/cli/postgres)
- DigitalOcean: pool modes transaction (default), session ("prepared statements, advisory locks, listen/notify"), statement; "clusters provide 25 connections per 1 GiB of RAM" with three reserved; `pg_dump` fails through a transaction-mode pool — [DigitalOcean connection pools](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/how-to/manage-connection-pools/); available backend connections: 1 GiB 22, 2 GiB 47, 4 GiB 97, 8 GiB 197, 16 GiB 397; 21 PgBouncer pools and up to 1,000 pooled connections — [DigitalOcean limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/)
- Prisma Postgres: pooled connection limits 10 (Free), 100 (Starter), 500 (Pro), 1,000 (Business); direct 10/10/50/100; "All plans use transaction pool mode with a 60-minute idle timeout" — [Prisma pricing](https://www.prisma.io/pricing)

### Inferences
- sqlx's protocol-level prepared statements are compatible with PgBouncer 1.21+/1.22+ transaction pools (Neon, Supabase dedicated pooler, Fly, Railway, DO when the bundled version is recent). On Supabase's shared Supavisor transaction port, sqlx would need statement caching disabled or the session port.
- Per-transaction tenant context via `SET LOCAL` or `set_config(..., true)` wrapped in `BEGIN ... COMMIT` is safe through any transaction-mode PgBouncer, because the setting dies with the transaction. On RDS Proxy the docs say `set_config` to "assign default values to session variables" pins; a transaction-scoped `set_config(..., true)` is not explicitly exempted for PostgreSQL, so RDS Proxy should be assumed to pin unless tested.
- LISTEN/NOTIFY needs one direct connection per hub instance on every provider. With two or three instances this is a handful of direct connections, well within even DigitalOcean's 22 on the 1 GiB node, as long as request handling goes through the pooler.
- For 25 s long-poll readers, a Rust server holding one pooled connection per in-flight poll would exhaust small direct limits quickly; the design should wait on an in-process broadcast fed by the single LISTEN connection and only hit the database on wake, which is how all these providers' limits stay comfortable.

### Gaps
- PgBouncer versions bundled by Crunchy, Fly, Railway, and DigitalOcean were not found; prepared-statement passthrough depends on 1.21+.
- Render's pooler mode and implementation were not found (the page only links to a separate article).
- Tiger Cloud's pooler mode and ports were not on the security page read.
- Whether RDS Proxy treats transaction-scoped `set_config(..., true)` as pinning is not stated for PostgreSQL.

## Key question 4: Backups and point-in-time recovery

### Takeaway
PITR with a 7-day or longer window and restore-to-new-instance is standard at RDS/Aurora (up to 35 days), Crunchy Bridge (10 days, included), Render (3 days Hobby, 7 days Pro+, included on paid instances), Railway (about 4 weeks, storage-cost only), DigitalOcean (7 days, included), Neon (7 days Launch, 30 days Scale, via history retention), Tiger Cloud (3 days Performance, 14 days Scale), and Fly.io (window length undocumented). Supabase charges $100/month per 7 days and needs Small compute; Prisma Postgres has daily snapshots only, with PITR promised for November 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Neon: history window for point-in-time restore is 6 hours on Free (1 GB limit), "Up to 7 days" on Launch, "Up to 30 days" on Scale; "Instant restore" costs $0.20/GB-month on paid plans; snapshots are $0.09/GB-month from 2026-05-01 — [Neon pricing](https://neon.com/pricing), [Neon docs plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans)
- Supabase: Pro has "Daily backups stored for 7 days", Team 14 days, Enterprise up to 30; PITR "$100 per month per 7 days retention" — [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing); WAL "at two-minute intervals", "PITR achieves a Recovery Point Objective (RPO) of two minutes"; PITR requires "at least a Small compute add-on"; 7 days $0.137/hour (about $100/month), 14 days $0.274/hour, 28 days $0.55/hour; "If you enable PITR, we will no longer take Daily Backups"; "The project is inaccessible during this process, so plan for downtime beforehand" — [Supabase backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups)
- Crunchy Bridge: "Physical backups are included with Crunchy Bridge clusters automatically"; 10 days retained; WAL streamed "every 60 seconds or 16MB"; restore "to any minute in its history" via "Fork from Backup" into a new cluster; backups past 10 days cannot be used for PITR; destroyed-cluster backups kept 5 days — [Crunchy Bridge backups](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/backups)
- RDS: "you can recover your DB instance to any point in time during the backup retention period"; free backup storage "equal to 100 percent of the total provisioned database storage"; up to 100 manual snapshots per Region — [RDS automated backups](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/USER_WorkingWithAutomatedBackups.html); third-party guides state the retention range is 1 to 35 days with a default of 7 — [Rapydo RDS pricing guide](https://www.rapydo.io/blog/aws-rds-pricing-a-comprehensive-guide) (third-party; the AWS page read did not include the range)
- Aurora: "no charge for backup storage up to 100% of cluster size" — [Aurora pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/rds/aurora/pricing/)
- Fly.io MPG: `fly mpg restore --pitr-time <RFC3339>` added in flyctl v0.4.80 (2026-08-10); "PITR restore is available for v2 clusters only"; "A restore always creates a new cluster, billed separately" — [Fly changelog, MPG PITR](https://fly.io/changelog/managed-postgres-point-in-time-restore), [fly mpg restore](https://fly.io/docs/flyctl/mpg-restore/)
- Render: PITR "Hobby: Past 3 days", "Pro or higher: Past 7 days"; "When you trigger PITR, Render spins up a new database instance"; "You can't restore to a time that's within ten minutes of the current time"; logical backups retained seven days; no recovery on the Free compute plan — [Render backups](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-backups)
- Railway: PITR archives "every WAL segment to a private Railway storage bucket using pgBackRest" with "rolling base backups (weekly full, daily incremental)"; "The last 4 full backups are retained, giving a restore window of roughly 4 weeks"; "PITR has no separate fee", bucket storage "$0.015 per GB-month"; PITR restores to a new service named `<source>-restored-YYYYMMDD-HHMM`; volume backups daily kept 6 days, weekly kept 1 month, monthly kept 3 months — [Railway backups and restores](https://railway.com/guides/postgres-backups-restores)
- DigitalOcean: "Point-in-time-recovery (PITR) is limited to the last 7 days"; restore must go to a new node — [DigitalOcean limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/)
- Tiger Cloud: Performance "Point-in-time-recovery to 3 days"; Scale "Point-in-time-recovery to 14 days" — [Tiger pricing](https://www.tigerdata.com/pricing)
- Prisma Postgres: daily snapshots on Starter/Pro/Business "taken only on days with activity", retained 7 or 30 days; "Point-in-time recovery is not currently available; the page says it is coming in November 2026" — [Prisma Postgres overview](https://www.prisma.io/docs/postgres/introduction/overview), [Prisma pricing](https://www.prisma.io/pricing)

### Inferences
- The cheapest always-on configurations that include PITR at or above 7 days without an add-on are Crunchy Bridge (10 days, from $9 Hobby-0 but Hobby is "not intended for production"), Render Pro workspace (7 days), DigitalOcean ($15, 7 days), and RDS (35 days configurable). Supabase's $100/month PITR add-on roughly quintuples the cost of a small Pro project.
- Only Supabase documents an in-place PITR restore with downtime; everyone else restores to a new instance, which is the safer model for a hub that must stay online while a restore is inspected.

### Gaps
- Fly.io MPG's PITR window length is not published.
- Restore times (minutes to completion) are not published by any provider except Crunchy's note that non-HA recovery "could be a matter of minutes" for small clusters and "many hours" for large ones — [Crunchy HA](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/high-availability).
- Xata's backup and PITR behaviour is not on its pricing page.
- Aurora's backup retention range (1 to 35 days) was not re-read from an AWS page in this session.

## Key question 5: High availability, failover, maintenance windows and forced restarts

### Takeaway
Synchronous standby with automatic failover is a priced add-on (roughly doubling compute) at RDS Multi-AZ, Crunchy Bridge (non-Hobby only), DigitalOcean (standby billed at primary rate; Standard Edition loses standby support from 2026-10-15 for new accounts), Tiger Cloud, and Render (asynchronous, few-second failover after 30 s unavailability). Fly.io MPG and Railway HA (Patroni) include multi-node HA in every plan. Neon has no standby in the classic sense (compute is stateless over safekeepers and pageservers) and Supabase has none below Enterprise beyond read replicas. Only AWS publishes recovery figures (Aurora Global switchover under 30 s on recent versions; Global failover RTO "in the order of minutes").

### Cited Findings
- RDS Multi-AZ DB instance: "Amazon RDS automatically provisions and maintains a synchronous standby replica in a different Availability Zone"; "can enhance availability during planned system maintenance"; the standby cannot serve reads — [RDS Multi-AZ DB instance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Concepts.MultiAZSingleStandby.html); Multi-AZ DB cluster has two readable standbys — [RDS Multi-AZ](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Concepts.MultiAZ.html)
- RDS Proxy "connects to the standby database without dropping idle application connections" during failover; without the proxy "a failover involves a brief outage" — [RDS Proxy concepts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/rds-proxy.howitworks.html)
- Aurora: storage is "six copies of all your data, spread across three AZs"; up to 15 serverless readers across 3 AZs; readers in promotion tier 0 or 1 track writer capacity so "a reader is always ready to take over for the writer in case of a failover" — [Aurora serverless how it works](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2.how-it-works.html)
- Crunchy Bridge HA "creates a hidden hot standby which receives the same writes as your primary"; on failure the control plane "will promote the standby to replace the impacted cluster in an instant"; "your connection string does not change" but existing connections drop; "HA is not available on Hobby tier plans" — [Crunchy HA](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/high-availability); HA "doubles the cluster price" — [Crunchy plans and pricing](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/plans-pricing); the AWS page claims "failover times in seconds" — [Crunchy on AWS](https://crunchydata.com/cloud/aws)
- Fly.io MPG: "Every plan includes high availability, automated backups and connection pooling" — [Fly pricing](https://fly.io/pricing); "Minor versions are updated during your maintenance window"; "Email notices cover maintenance only. There are no alerts for CPU or storage" — [Fly MPG overview](https://docs.fly.io/mpg/overview); a community post says the cheapest tier "is HA meaning its 2 nodes by default" — [Fly community](https://community.fly.io/t/managed-postgres-pricing/25734) (community source)
- Render HA: failover is triggered automatically after the primary "becomes unavailable for 30 seconds" and "takes a few seconds"; "data is replicated asynchronously" so recent writes can be lost; the standby matches the primary's plan and "is billed accordingly"; "The standby runs in the same region as the primary"; "Enabling HA requires a database restart" (under five minutes); requires a compute plan with at least 1 CPU — [Render HA](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability)
- Railway HA: a service converts to a cluster "backed by Patroni, etcd, and HAProxy" with automatic failover — [Railway PostgreSQL guide](https://docs.railway.com/guides/postgresql); CLI flags `--replicas`, `--coordinators` ("Must be odd"), `--edge`; "Switchover causes brief downtime" — [Railway CLI postgres](https://docs.railway.com/cli/postgres); enabling PITR on HA "rolls through the cluster with about 5 seconds of unavailability" — [Railway backups](https://railway.com/guides/postgres-backups-restores); HA is open to all plans per a 2026-03-27 changelog cited in a forum answer — [Railway Station](https://station.railway.com/questions/is-postgres-high-availability-available-14166ab5) (community source)
- DigitalOcean: HA clusters "begin at $30.00 per month for a 2 GiB RAM/1 vCPU primary node" plus "at least one $30.00 per month matching standby node for automatic failover"; "Standby nodes for high availability and read-only nodes bill at the same rate as the primary node"; "new Standard Edition clusters cannot use plans with more than 4 GiB of RAM or add standby or read-only nodes" from 2026-10-15 (new accounts) and 2026-11-30 (all accounts) — [DigitalOcean PostgreSQL pricing](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/pricing/); each cluster limited to 3 nodes; standby nodes must be in the same region — [DigitalOcean limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/)
- Tiger Cloud: Performance "Single-node high-availability replicas", Scale "Multi-node high-availability replicas"; "HA and read replicas are both charged at the same rate as your primary service" — [Tiger pricing](https://www.tigerdata.com/pricing)
- Supabase: the pricing page read does not list an HA/standby option on Free, Pro, or Team; auto-scaling compute is "available only on Enterprise" per third-party summaries of the pricing page — [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)
- Neon: compute suspends and resumes; "Safekeepers store primary changes durably" and replicas start on demand — [Neon read replicas](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/read-replicas); no standby/failover RTO figures found on the pricing or docs pages read.

### Inferences
- For the hub's "one small instance" stage, Fly.io MPG ($38 Basic with HA included) and Railway (HA on any plan, pay-per-resource) are the only candidates where HA does not double the bill. For the "fail-safe" stage, RDS Multi-AZ or Aurora with a tier-0 reader give the best-documented behaviour, at the cost of two instances.
- DigitalOcean's late-2026 restriction means HA on DO now requires Advanced Edition from $130/month plus a $130 standby, which removes DO from the cheap-HA shortlist.
- Render's asynchronous HA means a non-zero RPO on failover; for a change log that must not lose rows, prefer synchronous options (RDS Multi-AZ, Crunchy hot standby) or accept the trade-off explicitly.

### Gaps
- No provider other than AWS publishes a numeric single-region failover RTO; Crunchy says "in an instant" and Render "a few seconds" without figures.
- Neon's and Supabase's compute-failure recovery time is not documented on the pages read.
- Maintenance notification practice (lead time, channels) is documented only by Fly ("Email notices cover maintenance only") and Tiger (maintenance window for minors; manual majors) on the pages read; RDS maintenance windows are well known but the maintenance page was not read this session.

## Key question 6: Multi-region (read replicas elsewhere, cross-region failover, global database, path from single writer)

### Takeaway
Only AWS offers a managed cross-region failover primitive: Aurora Global Database (RPO in seconds, RTO in minutes, planned switchover with RPO 0 and under 30 s on recent engine versions) and RDS cross-region read replicas with manual promotion. Neon, Render, Crunchy (per the pages read), DigitalOcean, and Fly keep replicas in-region; Neon's documented path to a second region is logical replication to a separate project. For a single-writer hub, the realistic migration path is: start anywhere, and when a second region is required move to Aurora (or RDS plus cross-region replica) in the same AWS region family.

### Cited Findings
- Aurora Global Database: "RTO can be in the order of minutes"; "RPO is typically measured in seconds"; switchover "RPO is 0 (no data loss)" and on Aurora PostgreSQL 16.8, 15.12, 14.17, 13.20 or higher "switchover recovery time for read/write operations is typically under 30 seconds"; managed failover requires matching major and minor engine versions; the global writer endpoint keeps its value across switchover; `rds.global_db_rpo` enforces an upper bound from 20 seconds and blocks commits when all secondaries exceed it; after an unplanned failover Aurora rebuilds secondaries, which "can take a few minutes to several hours" — [Aurora Global Database switchover and failover](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-global-database-disaster-recovery.html)
- Aurora serverless can be used in Global Database secondary clusters "so they're ready to take over during disaster recovery" — [Aurora serverless how it works](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2.how-it-works.html); but the primary writer and secondary clusters of a Global Database do not auto-pause — [Aurora auto-pause](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2-auto-pause.html)
- Aurora Global Database replicated write I/O is charged at $0.20 per million — [Aurora pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/rds/aurora/pricing/)
- RDS for PostgreSQL supports cross-Region cascading read replicas (walkthroughs require 14.1 or higher) — [RDS cross-Region cascading replicas](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/USER_PostgreSQL.Replication.ReadReplicas.Xregion.html); Blue/Green deployments "don't support cross-Region read replicas" — [RDS Blue/Green considerations](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/blue-green-deployments-considerations.html)
- Neon: "Neon only supports creating read replicas in the same region as your database"; for cross-region, "create a Neon project in another region and replicate data to it with logical replication"; replicas add no storage cost and count toward CU-hours — [Neon read replicas](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/read-replicas), [Neon pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)
- Supabase read replicas (2023 launch): "You can create read replicas in any of our 12 supported regions", up to two per project at launch, billed as compute plus storage plus 9 cents per GB of WAL transfer — [Supabase read replicas launch](https://supabase.com/blog/introducing-read-replicas) (2023; a third-party August 2026 report says limits are now two or five depending on compute, replicas require Small compute and PG 15+ — [ecorpit.com](https://ecorpit.com/supabase-read-replica-cost-model-throughput-price-conflict-2026/), unverified)
- Render: "The standby runs in the same region as the primary" — [Render HA](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability)
- DigitalOcean: "Standby nodes must be in the same region as the cluster" — [DigitalOcean limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/)
- Crunchy Bridge HA is "configurable for primary and read replica clusters"; cross-region replicas are not covered on the HA page — [Crunchy HA](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/high-availability)
- Railway volumes "follow the region of the service to which they are attached" and moving a volume between regions "causes downtime" — [Railway regions](https://docs.railway.com/reference/regions)

### Inferences
- A hub that may later need a second region should either start on Aurora PostgreSQL (so adding a secondary region is an `add-region` operation on the same cluster) or keep its schema portable and plan a one-time migration into Aurora. Starting on RDS gives a cheaper intermediate step (cross-region read replica, manual promotion, DNS switch) at the cost of a manual, higher-RTO failover.
- Supabase is the only non-AWS candidate with documented cross-region read replicas, but without a managed cross-region promotion it is read-scaling, not DR.
- Neon's logical-replication path means a second-region copy is an application-managed replica, with no automatic failover and with the usual logical-replication caveats (no DDL replication, sequences not replicated).

### Gaps
- Crunchy Bridge's read-replica docs (cross-region or cross-cloud) were not read; the HA page does not mention regions.
- Tiger Cloud's read-replica region constraints were not found.
- Fly.io MPG cross-region replication is not documented on the pages read.
- Supabase's current read-replica count limits and per-region availability rest on a third-party report.

## Key question 7: Branching or cloning for per-PR preview environments and cost of short-lived branches

### Takeaway
Neon, Supabase, and Xata have first-class branching; Neon's is copy-on-write with data, billed at $1.50 per branch-month beyond 10 included (prorated hourly) plus compute at $0.106/CU-hour when a branch runs; Supabase branches start empty by default (schema from migrations, data by seed or an "Include data" option) at $0.01344 per branch-hour; Xata branches share storage with no per-branch fee. Everyone else offers restore-to-new-instance (Crunchy "Fork from Backup", Railway PITR fork, Render PITR, Aurora clone), which is a full instance billed as such.

This is the cheapest documented preview-environment model among the candidates.
- Supabase branches cost $0.32/day each while they exist; forgetting to delete a branch for a month costs about $9.60, and branches start without data, which suits schema and migration tests more than data-dependent tests.
- For the b4 hub's two fixed environments (prod and test), branching is a convenience rather than a requirement; the test hub can be a second small instance on any provider.

### Gaps
- Aurora clone and RDS snapshot-restore timing and pricing were not read in this session.

## Key question 8: Network (public TLS endpoint, IP allowlists, private networking to Fly/Render/Railway/AWS)

### Takeaway
All candidates expose a TLS public endpoint; Render and Tiger enforce TLS ("Render rejects external connections that set sslmode=disable"; Tiger has "no option to use unencrypted plaintext connections"). IP allowlists exist at Neon (IP Allow), Render (CIDR rules), Tiger, and (not re-read) RDS security groups. Private paths: Neon has AWS PrivateLink on the Scale plan (same region as the client); Tiger has VPC peering and PrivateLink; RDS/Aurora live in your VPC; Fly MPG, Railway, and Render databases are private to their own platform networks by default, so reaching them from another platform means a public endpoint or a tunnel.

### Cited Findings
- Neon Private Networking "is available on Neon's Scale plan", via an AWS endpoint service "available only within the same AWS region as your client application", maximum "10 private networking configurations per AWS region"; IP Allow restricts public access — [Neon private networking](https://neon.com/docs/guides/neon-private-networking), [Neon IP Allow](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/ip-allow); an older Neon blog placed the feature on Business and Enterprise plans — [Neon blog, PrivateLink](https://neon.com/blog/aws-privatelink-for-neon-databases) (docs are more recent)
- Supabase direct connections are IPv6 unless the IPv4 add-on swaps the AAAA record for an A record; the shared pooler is IPv4 on every plan — [Supabase connecting](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/connecting-to-postgres)
- Render: "External connections to your database are always encrypted in transit using Render-managed TLS certificates"; "Render rejects external connections that set sslmode=disable"; internal connections "do not support sslmode=verify-ca or sslmode=verify-full"; by default the instance "is accessible from any IP address" until CIDR rules are set; `--clear-ip-allow-list` disables external access — [Render creating and connecting](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-creating-connecting)
- Railway: "Databases are deployed private by default"; public access via Settings → Networking → Public Access populates `DATABASE_PUBLIC_URL` and "Network Egress is billed" — [Railway PostgreSQL guide](https://docs.railway.com/guides/postgresql)
- Fly.io MPG: "Clusters run inside your organization's private network"; public access is not described — [Fly MPG overview](https://docs.fly.io/mpg/overview)
- Tiger Cloud: "Customer access to Tiger Cloud services is only provided over TLS-encrypted connections"; VPC peering "only enables communication to be initiated from your Customer VPC"; PrivateLink "without crossing the public internet or requiring VPC peering"; IP allow lists supported — [Tiger security overview](https://www.tigerdata.com/docs/use-timescale/latest/security/overview)
- RDS Proxy: "Require Transport Layer Security" setting; supports TLS 1.0 to 1.3; IAM authentication available — [RDS Proxy concepts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/rds-proxy.howitworks.html)
- Prisma Postgres direct connections use `db.prisma.io:5432` with `sslmode=require` — [Prisma Postgres overview](https://www.prisma.io/docs/postgres/introduction/overview)

### Inferences
- If the hub binary runs on Fly, Render, or Railway, the lowest-friction database is that platform's own (private network, no egress charge). Pairing a Fly/Render/Railway app with Neon, Supabase, Crunchy, Tiger, or DO means a public TLS endpoint plus IP allowlisting (platform egress IPs are shared or dynamic, which weakens allowlists) or a Tailscale/WireGuard sidecar, which none of the database vendors document.
- If the hub runs on AWS (ECS/Fargate or App Runner), RDS/Aurora in the same VPC, Neon over PrivateLink (Scale plan), and Tiger over PrivateLink are the private options.

### Gaps
- No vendor documents a supported Tailscale or WireGuard path; this is a customer-built tunnel on every provider.
- RDS security-group and VPC details were not re-read this session (well established, but no URL captured).
- Crunchy Bridge network controls (firewall rules, VPC peering) were not read.
- DigitalOcean trusted sources and VPC behaviour were not read.

## Key question 9: Scale-to-zero / autosuspend and cold-start latency; disabling for prod

### Takeaway
Scale-to-zero exists at Neon (5 minute default; can be disabled on Launch, configurable from 1 minute to always-on on Scale), Aurora Serverless v2 (0 ACU with a 300 s to 86,400 s idle timer; typical resume about 15 s, 30 s or more after 24 h paused), Xata (branch compute suspends), and Prisma (60-minute pooled idle timeout implied). Supabase, Crunchy, RDS provisioned, Fly, Render, Railway, DO, and Tiger are always-on. For prod, Neon Launch disables autosuspend; Aurora sets minimum ACU 0.5 or higher.

### Cited Findings
- Neon: Free "Suspends after 5 min; cannot be turned off"; Launch "Suspends after 5 min; can be disabled"; Scale "Configurable, from 1 minute to always on" — [Neon pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)
- Aurora: "The minimum interval that you can set is 300 seconds (five minutes). That's the default"; maximum 86,400 seconds; "the typical time to resume might be approximately 15 seconds, we recommend that you adjust any client timeout settings to be longer than 15 seconds"; paused more than 24 hours "the resume time can be 30 seconds or longer"; "Any parameter changes that are in pending-reboot changes are applied when the instance resumes"; a cluster with logical replication, RDS Proxy, Global Database, or any provisioned instance does not pause; auto-pause requires Aurora PostgreSQL 16.3, 15.7, 14.12, or 13.15 or higher; turn off by setting minimum capacity "0.5 or greater" — [Aurora auto-pause](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2-auto-pause.html)
- Aurora: "Aurora serverless offers capacity from 0 ACUs to 256 ACUs for Aurora MySQL and to 384 ACUs for Aurora PostgreSQL"; each ACU is "approximately 2 gibibytes (GiB) of memory"; scaling in increments "as small as 0.5 ACUs" — [Aurora serverless how it works](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/AuroraUserGuide/aurora-serverless-v2.how-it-works.html)
- Xata: Open Source "Wake-up time is about 10s"; Xata Cloud "Wake-up time is about 1s"; "Scale-to-zero suspends idle compute" — [Xata pricing](https://xata.io/pricing)
- Neon status trackers logged a "brief cold-start slowdown" in us-east-2 in March 2026 and a May 8 2026 incident where "roughly 1,000 projects in US-East-1 Cell 6 had slow or stuck compute starts for about two hours" — [IsDown Neon incident](https://isdown.app/status/neon/incidents/584317-database-connectivity-issues-with-projects-in-us-east-1-cell-6) (third-party tracker)
- Supabase, Crunchy Bridge, Render, Railway, DigitalOcean, Tiger Cloud, and Fly.io MPG price fixed monthly compute with no suspend option on the pricing pages read — [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), [Crunchy pricing](https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/plans-pricing), [Render backups](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-backups), [Railway plans](https://docs.railway.com/reference/pricing/plans), [DigitalOcean pricing](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/pricing/), [Tiger pricing](https://www.tigerdata.com/pricing), [Fly pricing](https://fly.io/pricing)

### Inferences
- For the test hub, Neon Launch with autosuspend on or Aurora Serverless v2 at 0 ACU can cost a few dollars a month of compute; the hub's 25 s long-poll clients will keep a Neon compute awake while any test client is connected, so suspension only saves money between test runs.
- For prod, both Neon ("can be disabled" on Launch) and Aurora (minimum 0.5 ACU) have a documented always-on switch; Aurora's 15 s resume is incompatible with a 25 s long-poll budget only if the hub also has to connect cold, so prod should never pause.
- Neon's cold-start incidents in 2026 are a reason to keep prod always-on even though the feature exists.

### Gaps
- Neon does not publish a typical cold-start latency figure on the pricing or pooling pages read.
- Prisma Postgres does not document compute suspension; the 60-minute idle timeout applies to pooled connections.
