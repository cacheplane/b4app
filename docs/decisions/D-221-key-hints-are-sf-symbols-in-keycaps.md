## 2026-11-13 (key hints)

- **D-221 A key hint on the Mac is the key's SF Symbol in a small drawn cap; Tab is `arrow.right.to.line.compact`; no key or shortcut changes.** At the owner's direction, after seeing four renders of the footer ("Let's go back to the SF symbol. And I'd like to update all the icons accordingly. To SF symbol. And let's use the compact variant specifically in this case"; earlier: "I want to keep the tab key, and current keyboard shortcuts, do not change those"). The research is `docs/research/2026-11-13-key-symbols-and-icon-packs.md`.
  - **What the research found.** Apple's guidelines give glyphs and an order only for the four modifiers, and say nothing about key hints inside a custom view or about keycaps; the Tab, Return and Escape glyphs appear only on its support pages. SF Symbols has every key the palette names (`command`, `shift`, `option`, `control`, `return`, `escape`, `arrow.right.to.line`) but a boxed variant only for a few, so the cap is drawn by the app. An icon pack draws Tab as the same arrow meeting a bar and loses the weight matching, baseline and dark-mode behaviour of the system's symbols, so none is added.
  - **The cap.** `KeyCap` (`Sources/WorkspaceUI/Theme/KeyCap.swift`): the symbol at 10.5 pt medium in the muted colour (a printed key is its character at 11 pt medium), 5 pt either side, at least 20 by 18 pt, a 4.5 pt continuous corner, the surface fill and a 1 pt border in the ghost colour at 70%. `KeyCaps` sets the caps of one shortcut 3 pt apart. `HintKey` is the key: its symbol, its name in words, and `keys(in:)`, which reads a label written in the menu bar's glyphs, so `CommandDefinition.shortcutLabel` is unchanged. The compact Tab arrow is used because the full one touches the cap's edges at this size.
  - **Where.** The palette's footer (Actions: Tab; Back: Shift and Tab; the primary action: Return), the field row's Escape (which was the word "esc" in the mono face), a command row's shortcut, the title bar's resting control (Command and K), and the composer's `/` list (Return, and a command's shortcut, which was a string in the row's value slot and is now its own field). The bare glyphs in the mono face are gone from all of them. The research recommended keeping Escape as a word and offered a redrawn Tab; the owner chose the system's symbol for every key, so Escape is `escape`.
  - **Rows.** A command's leading symbol is 13 pt regular in the muted colour (it was 12 pt light in the ghost colour, fainter than the text it labels). A Mac palette row that Tab opens actions for ends in a chevron, as the phone's does.
  - **Saying it.** The caps are hidden from VoiceOver; the button that carries them says the key in words ("Actions, Tab", "Back, Shift-Tab", "Rename, Return"), and a row with a shortcut says it ("Shift-Command-P"). The footer hints and the Escape cap have tooltips naming the key.
  - **Tests.** `HintKeyTests`: a label's glyphs become keys in order, every named key's symbol exists on the system, and every command's label is modifiers then one printed key. The snapshot suite's palette pages and `/` list were rendered in light and dark and looked over. Not checked on a real screen, with VoiceOver, or on a system older than the one the compact symbol shipped in.
