## 2026-11-09 (the relay's last small gaps)

Two low-priority findings from reading Phoenix's source against the hub (`docs/research/2026-11-03-phoenix-source-hardening/socket_layer.md`, L2 and L5) that the earlier rounds (D-162 to D-179) left open. Numbered D-192 by `Scripts/docs-index.sh --next`.

- **D-192 A link leaves the registry however its task ends, and a repeated `watch` costs no lookup.**
  - **Cleanup.** `serve_link` took the link out of the registry only when it returned normally, so a panic in the link path (none is known) would have left the device online, with its watches, until it linked again; Phoenix's channels die with their transport through monitors. The registration is now held by a guard, `Registered` in `relay/link.rs`: the ordinary end still unregisters in its place in the cleanup (after the last-seen write, before the claim release and the presence broadcast), and a task that panics or is dropped first is unregistered on drop, with the claim release (D-172) and, for a Mac, the presence broadcast run on a task of their own. The last-seen write is skipped in that case; the device's last minute's write stands.
  - **Watch.** A `watch` of a thread the link already watches changed nothing but still looked the thread's owner up, one database round trip per frame for a client that repeats its watches. `handle_watch` now answers nothing at once when `Relay::watches` says the link (still its device's current one) has the thread. A watched thread deleted since is therefore not reported `not_found` on a repeat; it simply sends no events. `watch` stays unlimited by rate: a link's watches are handled one at a time in its read loop, so a looping client holds at most one pool connection.
  - **Tests.** `relay.rs`: `a_link_task_that_panics_still_takes_its_device_offline` (through `Relay::panic_on_next_frame`, a test seam) and `watching_a_thread_again_needs_no_lookup` (the thread's row deleted after the first watch). Each failed with its fix switched off.
