## 2026-11-12 (command centre)

- **D-215 The command palette rests in the middle of the Mac's title bar and lifts out of it; its panel is the card's own size, with the system's shadow, and snaps home (amends D-207 and D-214).** The owner asked for the palette "in" the header as in VS Code, for a grab by the header, for a snap back to centre, and reported that the D-214 panel's shadow lagged. The research behind this is `reports/Title bar command palette on macOS.md` (not committed); the owner chose a button centred on the whole window, a palette that lifts on a click, and snapping only near home.
  - **The button** (`CommandCenterButton`, `Sources/WorkspaceUI/Palette/CommandCenter.swift`). A 360 by 28 pt capsule in the middle of the title bar: a search glyph, "Search or run a command", and ⌘K. It is a button, as VS Code's is, not a field: a click (or ⌘K) opens the palette, whose field is drawn where the button was, and the button is blank while the palette is up. The thread's title leaves the bar (`.toolbar(removing: .title)`); it is the heading under it, and still the window's name. The thread's own buttons keep the trailing edge behind a flexible spacer.
  - **Not a toolbar item.** Tried first, on a real window: a SwiftUI `.principal` item is centred on the detail column, not the window, and with this button as its content the toolbar moved it, and the sidebar's own three buttons with it, into the overflow menu at 360, 200, and flexible widths, with and without the shared glass background (a bare text button did show). A view drawn into the title bar from the content takes no clicks there. So `CommandCenterPresenter` puts the button in a small borderless panel that is a child of the window, centred across it and in the middle of the title bar's height, laid out again whenever the window updates. It never takes the keyboard; a click on it brings the app forward. It is unseen in full screen, under a sheet, in a window too narrow for it, and while nobody is signed in.
  - **Home.** The palette opens centred on the button with the button's middle 24 pt under the palette's top, so the 48 pt field row covers it. Where there is no button (full screen, a narrow window) it opens 96 pt under the window's top, as before.
  - **The panel is the card** (`FloatingPanel`). D-214's panel was a fixed transparent window larger than the card, with the shadow drawn by SwiftUI; the shadow was redrawn on every frame of the card's height animation and trailed it. Now the panel is exactly the content's size, follows each new height at once with its top held (the card's height is no longer animated in a panel), rounds its own corners, and has the window server's shadow, asked for again after each resize has drawn. The place palette's panel is the same.
  - **Dragging.** The panel moves itself (`PalettePanel.sendEvent`), which is what lets it know when a drag ends. A drag starts on a `WindowDragArea` (the header's and the footer's empty stretches) or on the empty part of a text field, past the end of its text, once the pointer has travelled 3 pt. A press on the field that never becomes a drag puts the cursor at the end of the text. Text in the field still selects, and rows and buttons keep their clicks.
  - **Snapping.** Released within 20 pt of home on an axis (VS Code's figure), the panel goes home on that axis: onto the centre line, onto the button's height, or both. Elsewhere it stays until it closes. A double click on a drag area sends it home, and it opens at home every time; D-214's remembered position is gone.
  - **Focus.** The panel puts the cursor in its content's field itself once it is shown. SwiftUI's own request could arrive before the panel had the keyboard and be lost, which left the palette open with nowhere to type.
  - **Tests.** `FloatingPanelGeometryTests` (the release rule, keeping on screen) and `FloatingPanelTests` (content-sized with the top held, recentred when the window resizes, the release rule and the reset on a real panel, home on every open). On a real screen, `PaletteSmokeTests`: the button is centred in the window with the sidebar open and hidden and clear of the thread's buttons; a click opens the palette over it with the cursor in it; the palette drags by the field's empty part and by the footer, a plain click there is not a nudge, a release near home snaps onto the button, a double click goes home; and screen pixels show a shadow under the card and nothing left where a taller card was after the list shrinks or the palette closes.
  - **Not checked.** Full screen, a second display, a sheet over the window, VoiceOver, and dark mode on a real screen; whether the button's panel is ever left in front of another app's window.
