## 2026-11-12 (command centre)

- **D-216 The command palette rests in the middle of the Mac's title bar and lifts out of it; its panel is the card's own size, with the system's shadow, and snaps home (amends D-207 and D-215).** The owner asked for the palette "in" the header as in VS Code, for a grab by the header, for a snap back to centre, and reported that the D-215 panel's shadow lagged. The research behind this is `reports/Title bar command palette on macOS.md` (not committed); the owner chose a button centred on the whole window, a palette that lifts on a click, and snapping only near home.
  - **The button** (`CommandCenterButton`, `Sources/WorkspaceUI/Palette/CommandCenter.swift`). A 360 by 28 pt capsule in the middle of the title bar: a search glyph, "Search or run a command", and ⌘K. It is a button, as VS Code's is, not a field: a click (or ⌘K) opens the palette, whose field is drawn where the button was, and the button is blank while the palette is up. The thread's title leaves the bar (`.toolbar(removing: .title)`); it is the heading under it, and still the window's name. The thread's own buttons keep the trailing edge behind a flexible spacer.
  - **Not a toolbar item.** Tried first, on a real window: a SwiftUI `.principal` item is centred on the detail column, not the window, and with this button as its content the toolbar moved it, and the sidebar's own three buttons with it, into the overflow menu at 360, 200, and flexible widths, with and without the shared glass background (a bare text button did show). A view drawn into the title bar from the content takes no clicks there. So `CommandCenterPresenter` puts the button in a small borderless panel that is a child of the window, centred across it and in the middle of the title bar's height, laid out again whenever the window updates. It never takes the keyboard; a click on it brings the app forward. It is unseen in full screen, under a sheet, in a window too narrow for it, and while nobody is signed in.
  - **Home.** The palette opens centred on the button with the button's middle 24 pt under the palette's top, so the 48 pt field row covers it. Where there is no button (full screen, a narrow window) it opens 96 pt under the window's top, as before.
  - **The panel is the card** (`FloatingPanel`). D-215's panel was a fixed transparent window larger than the card, with the shadow drawn by SwiftUI; the shadow was redrawn on every frame of the card's height animation and trailed it. Now the panel is exactly the content's size, follows each new height at once with its top held (the card's height is no longer animated in a panel), rounds its own corners, and has the window server's shadow, asked for again after each resize has drawn. The place palette's panel is the same.
  - **Dragging.** The panel moves itself (`PalettePanel.sendEvent`), which is what lets it know when a drag ends. A drag starts on a `WindowDragArea` (the header's and the footer's empty stretches) or on the empty part of a text field, past the end of its text, once the pointer has travelled 3 pt. A press on the field that never becomes a drag puts the cursor at the end of the text. Text in the field still selects, and rows and buttons keep their clicks.
  - **Snapping.** Released within 20 pt of home on an axis (VS Code's figure), the panel goes home on that axis: onto the centre line, onto the button's height, or both. Elsewhere it stays until it closes. A double click on a drag area sends it home, and it opens at home every time; D-215's remembered position is gone.
  - **Focus.** The panel puts the cursor in its content's field itself once it is shown. SwiftUI's own request could arrive before the panel had the keyboard and be lost, which left the palette open with nowhere to type.
  - **Tests.** `FloatingPanelGeometryTests` (the release rule, keeping on screen) and `FloatingPanelTests` (content-sized with the top held, recentred when the window resizes, the release rule and the reset on a real panel, home on every open). On a real screen, `PaletteSmokeTests`: the button is centred in the window with the sidebar open and hidden and clear of the thread's buttons; a click opens the palette over it with the cursor in it; the palette drags by the field's empty part and by the footer, a plain click there is not a nudge, a release near home snaps onto the button, a double click goes home; and screen pixels show a shadow under the card and nothing left where a taller card was after the list shrinks or the palette closes.
  - **Not checked.** Full screen, a second display, a sheet over the window, VoiceOver, and dark mode on a real screen; whether the button's panel is ever left in front of another app's window.
  - **The resting control is the palette's own top row (same day, the owner's correction).** Not a 360 by 28 pt capsule: the owner asked that it "look pixel identical to the top of the command palette, not elevated". It is now the open palette's field row drawn flat: 48 pt high, the card's corner radius and edge, the same glyph, Find's prompt in the field's own placeholder colour, and ⌘K where the open palette says "esc". It is the palette's 600 pt wide in a window of 1140 pt or more and narrower below that (`CommandCenterButton.width(inWindow:)` keeps 540 pt of the title bar for its own buttons, down to 280 pt), so in a narrow window the palette opens wider than it. It keeps its text while the palette is up, since the palette covers it exactly. `PaletteSmokeTests` compares the screen at rest and with the palette open along three lines through the row and allows only the cursor to differ; `CommandCenterTests` covers the width rule.
  - **Snapping is gentler and its two axes are separate (same day, the owner's correction).** A 20 pt pull on release "feels very eager", and the owner wants to "snap it to the middle horizontally but also still easily slide it along". The snap is now applied while the panel is dragged, not on release, within 8 pt (`FloatingPanelGeometry.snapped`): near the centre line the panel sits on that line and still moves freely up and down it; near home's height it sits at that height and still moves freely across; only where the two cross is it home. The panel's place is measured from the press each time, so a held panel leaves a line as soon as the pointer has carried it 8 pt off, with no extra tug. Released, it stays where the drag left it. This replaces the 20 pt release rule above. `FloatingPanelGeometryTests` and `FloatingPanelTests` cover the rule; `PaletteSmokeTests` drags a real panel short of home (left alone), onto the centre line lower down (held across, free in height), up the line to home, and across at home's height (held in height, free across).
  - **The snap is a light pull to the window's two centre lines (same day, the owner's second correction).** The 8 pt lock was "still very aggressive and not behaving on both axis": it locked instead of drawing, and its line across was the palette's home height, not the window's middle. The owner's picture is a line down the middle of the window and a line across its middle; only near a line should the palette want to go to it, lightly, and most where they cross. Now `FloatingPanelGeometry.drawn` shrinks the panel's distance from a line it is within 24 pt of (24 × (distance ÷ 24) ^ 1.6: about 4 pt of pull at 12 pt out, on the line under 3 pt), with no jump at the edge of the reach and no hold, so the panel drifts on and comes away as smoothly. The line down the middle draws it sideways and leaves it free up and down; the lines across draw it in height and leave it free from side to side. There are two lines across: the middle of the window (the palette at its tallest, centred) and where the palette opens, so it can still be put back over the title bar's control. This replaces the 8 pt rule above; released, the panel stays exactly where the drag left it. `FloatingPanelGeometryTests` cover the curve (out of reach untouched, light, monotonic, no jump) and the lines; `PaletteSmokeTests` drags a real panel to 12 pt from the centre line (drawn, not on it), onto it, along it to the middle of the window, across from there, and away from every line.
  - **The field row is 36 pt with a capsule's ends (same day; the owner chose it from three directions).** At 48 pt the resting control nearly filled the 52 pt title bar and matched nothing beside it: "the padding around the button / facade of the command palette header does not fit with the rest of the app". Rather than let the resting control differ from the open palette, both were slimmed together: `MacPalette.fieldHeight` is 36, the card's corner radius is half of it (18, circular), so the row alone is a capsule like the bar's own button groups and the open card keeps those corners; the panel's layer rounds the same way. The figures above that say 48 pt, 24 pt and the card's 16 pt corners are now 36, 18 and 18. The snapshot suite draws the resting control and the card; the real-screen comparison was not rerun for this change (macOS asked for the owner's password to enable UI automation).
  - **The drag was jittery because the layout fought it (same day, found from the owner's report).** `reposition`, which runs whenever the window updates, put the panel back at home plus the offset of the drag before, on every pass, while the pointer was moving it: the panel jumped between the two. The window's layout now leaves a panel alone while it is being dragged (`PalettePanel.isBeingDragged`), and a change of height during a drag holds the top where the pointer has it. The pointer's place is read from each event, not from `NSEvent.mouseLocation`, so a drag can be replayed: `aDragIsNeverFoughtByTheWindowsLayout` sends a press, forty drags with a window update and a resize notice after each, and a release, and requires the panel to move on at every step and be exactly under the pointer. It failed before the fix with the panel back at home after every step.
  - **A long argument hint no longer widens the Commands list.** A row's trailing text was drawn at its full width; a runtime's hint can be a line long (Claude Code's `/code-review`), which made the list wider than the card and ran every row off both edges. The trailing text is cut at 220 pt. `aLongArgumentHintIsCutInsideTheCard` draws the card and requires the row's margins to be empty; it fails on the old code.
  - **The pull is firmer, and the palette opens where it was left (2026-11-12, the owner's correction once the jitter was gone).** With the drag steady the 24 pt, exponent 1.6 pull was "too weak". A line now reaches 28 pt, the panel sits on it within 5 pt (`FloatingPanelGeometry.hold`: a short stretch of the pointer's travel that does not move it), and between the two its distance is `reach` × t², t from 0 at the hold to 1 at the edge, so at 20 pt it has been drawn 8 pt in and nothing jumps at either end. The two lines still act by themselves. The place the panel is left is kept, amending "it opens at home every time" (D-215 and above): `FloatingPanel` takes `offset` and `remember`, and the root view keeps the offset from home on this Mac in `LocalPreferences` (`PalettePlace`, key `palette.offset`; never synced). It is an offset from home, so it follows the window, and it is still clamped to the screen. A double click on a drag area goes home and forgets the place. The folder palette does not move and keeps nothing. `aPanelOpensWhereItWasLastLeft` covers it; not checked on a real screen.
  - **Stronger again, and where the panel opens is a point that draws it (2026-11-12, the owner's correction).** A line reaches 40 pt and holds the panel within 8 pt, with the same t² between. Where the panel opens (home, over the button) now draws it from every side as a point: within 72 pt it is drawn along the straight line to home (`drawn(_:towardPoint:)`), within 14 pt it is home, and the two lines then draw what the point leaves, so the result is continuous everywhere. In practice a panel let go within about 30 pt of home is home, from a diagonal as well as along a line, which the lines alone did not do. A drawn distance under half a point counts as none. `whereThePanelOpensDrawsItFromEverySide` covers the point; the drag replay now allows the hold at the start of a drag and still requires the panel never to go back. Not checked on a real screen.
  - **What the first CI run found (2026-11-12).** Three faults, none seen on the owner's Mac. (1) On CI's macOS 26.7 a press on the empty part of the palette's field never began a drag: `dragStart` asked the field's editor for its layout manager to find where the text ends, and an editor under TextKit 2 has none. The text's width is now measured from the text itself, and the field is found from whichever view inside it the press lands on; `theEmptyPartOfAFocusedFieldDragsThePanel` drags a panel by a focused SwiftUI field. (2) `appleSheetSaysItHasNoWindow` failed whenever a palette panel in another suite had the keyboard, because the sign-in sheets anchored to the key window whatever it was. A sheet on the palette would also be wrong in the app (the palette closes when it loses the keyboard), so `anchor()` takes the key window only when it can be a main window. (3) The real-screen palette tests assumed a desktop wider than CI's 1024 pt and still described the first, lighter snap: the drag test now runs in a window no wider than the desktop and follows the present figures and the remembered place, and the test that compares the resting control with the open palette edge on edge is skipped on a desktop under 1200 pt. Those two tests have not passed on a real screen since they were rewritten; CI's run of the first is their check.
  - **CI's second run: the layout manager was not it, or not all of it.** With the text measured from the text, the same drag by the field still left the palette at home on CI's screen (and the unit test that drags a panel by a focused field passed there, as it does on the owner's Mac). What the last passing real-screen run and the present code differ in, on every drag's path, is where the pointer is read from: the jitter fix took it from each event's place in the window so that a drag could be replayed in a test, where before it was `NSEvent.mouseLocation`. The panel reads the pointer itself again (`PalettePanel.pointer`), and a test that replays events supplies their places. This is a second guess, checked only by CI's next run; the owner's Mac dragged correctly with either.
  - **CI's fourth run found it: in the palette's header the press hits the hosting view.** Neither guess above was the cause. With a trace of each press (debug builds; `WindowDragArea.Handle`'s accessibility value, which the screen test prints when a drag does nothing), CI showed the press on the field landing on the `NSHostingView` itself: SwiftUI answers for every spot of the palette's header row, so `hitTest` returns neither the field nor the drag area behind it, on the owner's Mac as on CI's. Dragging by the footer's middle worked because nothing of SwiftUI's covers it. The simpler header the unit test used did not behave so, which is why the unit test passed. The panel now finds the field (its editor first) and the drag areas by where they are, and takes a press as the start of a drag when it hits the drag area, a text view, or the hosting view over a drag area. A press of the last kind that never becomes a drag is given back to SwiftUI, press then release, so a button in a footer still gets its click. `thePaletteIsDraggedByItsFieldAndItsHeader` drags the real `PaletteView` in its panel by the field's empty part, the header's leading end and the footer, checks a row does not drag it, and checks a click on the field's empty part leaves the cursor in the field; it fails on the code before. The pointer is still read from `NSEvent.mouseLocation`, and the text still measured from itself: both are sound, and neither was the fault.
