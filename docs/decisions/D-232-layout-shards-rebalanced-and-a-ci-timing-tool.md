## 2026-11-14 (CI: the layout shards are rebalanced and a tool reads a run's clocks; three other changes were tried on hosted runs and reverted)

- **D-232 `testSyncCopyJourneys` moves from shard 2 to shard 1; `Scripts/ci_timings.py` reads a run's clocks from GitHub; the two combining checks' shell is tested on failing outcomes. Building the layout fixture during the Simulator's boot, building the beta iOS app in layout shard 1, and compiling only the `arm64` Simulator slice in the two iOS app builds were each tried on hosted runs and reverted: the workflow is D-229's.** Measured on the four green first attempts with D-229's workflow (runs 43629968947, 43671966677, 43673503907, 43675337568): the longest macOS job ran 975, 960, 865 and 1,015 s, and it was shard 2 in three of them and the package job in one. This entry claims no speedup. The gate record (2026-11-14-ci-boot-overlap-and-balance) has the five hosted runs.
  - **The shards.** From the four runs' result bundles, the journeys of shard 1 took 222 to 287 s and those of shard 2 291 to 533 s: D-225 split them by times from before D-229's waits. `testSyncCopyJourneys` (45 to 77 s) moves to shard 1. On the two hosted runs with the new plan the sums were 268 and 401 s, then 278 and 408 s, where the old plan would have had 207 and 462 s, then 221 and 465 s. The plan is still checked as a partition of the whole contract by every shard (D-225); shard 1 now owes 70 checkpoints and shard 2 owes 59. Shard 2 is still the longer job: on run 43684652276 its tests took 556 s for 408 s of journeys, and the plan cannot move that.
  - **Tried and reverted: the fixture's build during the boot.** A shard waits for `simctl bootstatus` (67 to 165 s in 15 baseline shard logs) before it compiles the fixture (147 to 406 s). `Scripts/test-ios-layout.sh` was changed to build, then wait, then test. On run 43678912064 the boot did finish during the build (9 and 7 s of wait left), and the build beside it took 371 and 361 s: 380 and 368 s before the first test, against a baseline median of 323 s for the wait and the build in turn, and 360 and 353 s on the next run with the old order. The rule fixed before the run asked for a step shorter by at least half the boot wait. Not met; reverted.
  - **Tried and reverted: the beta iOS build in layout shard 1.** With both iOS builds the package job ran 817 to 960 s and was the longest job in one baseline run of four, so the beta build (92 to 135 s there) was made a step of shard 1, which reported its outcome to the builds' check through a matrix job output that only shard 1 set. The output arrived on both runs. The build took 209 and 191 s there, having lost the first iOS build's warm DerivedData, and it shortened the longest job in neither run (shard 1 itself was the longest in the first). Reverted.
  - **Tried and dropped locally.** Building an app in the DerivedData of the UI fixture that had just built the same package, to reuse its modules: Xcode ran the same compile tasks either way (macOS 186 and 186, iOS 116 and 116), so nothing is shared between two projects' builds of one package.
  - **What the runs say about the measure.** Steps nothing here touched differed between the two hosted runs by more than any change: the package job's tests and builds took 875 s on one and 570 s on the other (baseline 630 to 722 s). A change worth less than that cannot be judged on one or two runs.
  - **The required checks.** Unchanged in name and in what they combine. `Scripts/tests/test_ci_scope.py` now runs both combining checks' own shell on 20 outcomes that must fail (a failed, cancelled, skipped or missing build; a skip without the scope's no).
  - **Tried and reverted: the iOS app builds compile the `arm64` Simulator slice only.** The destination is the generic Simulator, which builds every module for `arm64` and for `x86_64`, the Simulator on an Intel Mac. The owner said on 2026-11-15 that nobody builds this app on one, so the two iOS build steps passed `ARCHS=arm64`. Locally the build ran 204 `SwiftCompile` tasks where the generic one ran 408, in 33 s against 76 s. The rule fixed before the hosted runs asked for the two steps together to be at least 25% under their baseline median (271 s; 232 to 314 s in four runs) in each of two runs. They took 202 and 222 s (runs 43762471549 and 43771721159): 25.5% and 18.1%. Not met; reverted at the owner's word. Both runs were below every baseline run and compiled nothing for `x86_64`, so the steps were probably 40 to 60 s shorter; halving the compile tasks does not halve a build. The setting is harmless and can come back on other grounds than this measure.
  - **Not done: larger macOS runners.** GitHub offers them only to organizations on its Team or Enterprise Cloud plans and bills them by the minute in public repositories too (its documentation, read 2026-11-15: 5-core M2 Pro at $0.102 a minute; the 12-core one is Intel). This organization is on the free plan and the repository is public, so the standard runners cost nothing. Trying them needs the owner to change the plan and to accept a bill; nothing here does.
  - **The tool.** `Scripts/ci_timings.py collect | summarize | steps` writes one row per job of each attempt and derives the run's span, the wait before the macOS jobs are created, each job's wait for a runner and run time, the wait after the last macOS job, and runner time by class. A job cancelled while it waited never started and is counted in no run time; a job a re-run did not run is in none of that attempt's clocks. The rows for 85 runs are in `docs/research/2026-11-14-ci-delivery/`, with the manifest the comparison was fixed by.
