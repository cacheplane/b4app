# M8c Relay Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** From the iPhone Simulator, pair with a QR link, then continue and start Codex, Claude, and local B4 threads that run on the Mac. Replies stream live, approvals are answered, and turns are stopped. A send to an offline Mac is refused with its last-seen time, and the threads stay browsable while it is gone.

**Architecture:**
- **Link.** Every joined device holds one WebSocket to the hub (`/v1/devices/self/link`). The Mac is a host; the phone is a client.
- **Commands.** The hub authorises five client commands, routes each to the thread's or project's owning Mac with a deadline, and returns the Mac's reply. The Mac runs them through `RelayExecutor` → `SessionService`, unchanged as the only writer.
- **Live progress.** The hub fans live events out only for watched threads and reports host presence.
- **State.** It still travels only through the change log. Nothing from the relay is written to the mirror.
- **Pairing.** A joined Mac mints a 5-minute enrollment code and shows it as a `b4app://pair` QR code.

**Tech Stack:**
- **Hub:** Rust 1.93.1 with axum 0.8.9 (adding the `ws` feature), sqlx 0.8.6, and tokio, with `tokio-tungstenite` as a test-only dependency.
- **Swift 6.2 package:** `WorkspaceSync` (`URLSessionWebSocketTask`), `WorkspaceMirror`, `Coordinator` (Network's `NWPathMonitor`, IOKit power assertions), and `WorkspaceUI` (CoreImage QR generation). `NIOWebSocket` is used only by a test target.
- **iOS app:** AVFoundation for the QR scanner.
- **Live gate:** the Compose hub and the iPhone 17 Pro Simulator.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-m8c-relay-design.md` (approved by the owner 2026-11-01). It builds on `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-m8-hub-design.md` and `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-m8b-remote-workspace-design.md`; read all three, plus `Hub/API.md`. Decisions D-054 to D-059 are already in `docs/decisions.md`.

## Global Constraints

**Link**
- Endpoint `GET /v1/devices/self/link`, with bearer auth before the upgrade and a `B4-Protocol: 1` request header (missing = 1). Role: `macos` → `host`; `ios` or `cli` → `client`. One link per device: a newer link closes the older one with an `error` frame `link_replaced` and close code 4000. Revocation closes with 4001; hub shutdown with 1001.
- Heartbeat: the device sends `ping` every 10 s; the hub answers `pong { hubTime }`. The hub closes a link silent for 30 s; a device treats 30 s of silence as dead.
- Backoff: 1 s doubling to 30 s with full jitter on the Mac (15 s cap on the phone), reset after 60 s connected. The Mac reconnects at once on wake and on a network path change. A 401 stops the link until `hub join`.
- `last_seen_at` is written on open, at most once a minute while open, and on close as the time of the link's last received frame.

**Commands**
- Methods, an allowlist on both hub and Mac: `turn.send`, `turn.interrupt`, `request.respond`, `thread.start`, `turn.lookup`. Only `client` links may send commands; anything else is `not_found`.
- Deadline = hub now + 10 s. Reply wait: 30 s for `turn.send` and `thread.start`, 15 s for the rest. `turn.interrupt` and `request.respond` are held up to `lastSeenAt + 60 s` for an offline owner seen within 60 s, at most 32 held per host. Never held: `turn.send`, `thread.start`.
- Pending-thread map after routing `thread.start`: `threadId → owner` for 300 s, and the caller auto-watches the new thread.
- New error codes, on the hub and as Swift `ErrorCode`: `device_offline` (`details: { deviceId, deviceName, lastSeenAt }`), `command_expired`, `relay_timeout`, `rate_limited` (`details: { retryAfterMs }`), `link_replaced`, `client_too_old`, `watch_limit`. Reused: `not_found`, `stale_runtime_request`.

**Limits**
- Commands per client device: 60 a minute, bursts of 20. `turn.send` + `thread.start` per user: 20 a minute. Link upgrades per device: 10 a minute (`429` before the upgrade).
- Watched threads per client link: 4. Frames: 64 KiB from a client, 256 KiB from a host. Outbound queue per link: 256 frames; when it is full, a client's `event` frames are dropped, then `stream_gap`. Pairing codes: 10 per user per hour.

**Live events**
- Forwarded kinds: `turn.acknowledged`, `turn.state_changed`, `turn.finished`, `message.started`, `message.delta`, `message.completed`, `tool.started`, `tool.completed`, `runtime_request.opened`, `runtime_request.resolved`. Only for watched threads, and only when the encoded frame is ≤ 256 KiB.

**Phone timings**
- A send is retried with the same `id` only within 30 s of the tap. `turn.lookup` runs no sooner than 30 s after the tap; on `in_flight` it asks again after 5 s.
- Stop and answer wait up to 90 s. The disconnect grace is 60 s from `lastSeenAt`.
- The `thread.start` placeholder lives 2 minutes and buffers up to 256 events.

**Pairing**
- `POST /v1/pairing-codes` (`macos` only) → `{ code, expiresAt, hubURL? }`, valid 5 minutes, single use.
- Link form: `b4app://pair?hub=<percent-encoded URL>&code=<8 chars>`. The phone always asks for confirmation before enrolling.

**Copy, verbatim**
- "Reconnecting to your Mac…"
- "<Mac name> is offline · last seen <time>"
- "Not confirmed: your Mac may not have received this."
- "Didn't reach your Mac"
- "Not confirmed while your Mac is offline"
- "Waiting for your Mac…"
- "Already answered"
- "Connecting to the hub…"
- "Your Mac has left the hub. Its threads come back when it joins again."
- "Pair this iPhone with the hub at <host>?"
- "This iPhone is already paired"
- "Paired with <name>"
- "Join a hub first"
- The refusal for unserved phone commands: "… is done on the Mac."

## Review Focus

1. **The Mac's clock is minutes off the hub's.** The deadline check must use the hub-clock offset from `hello` and `pong`, not the Mac's wall clock. A command with 8 s left, received on a Mac whose clock is 2 minutes behind, still runs; one 1 s past its deadline, on a Mac 2 minutes ahead, is still dropped. Pinned in Task 8 (`deadlineUsesTheHubClockNotTheMacs`).
2. **The hub restarts during a send.** Its in-flight map is lost, the phone's link drops, and the phone resends the same `id` within 30 s. The Mac returns the turn it already made, so the result is one turn. Pinned in Task 4 (`a_resend_after_hub_restart_reaches_the_mac_again`) and Task 8 (`resendAfterCompletionReturnsTheSameTurn`).
3. **The phone goes to the background mid-send.** The link closes. Back in the foreground within 30 s, the send is retried; after 30 s it stays "not confirmed" and resolves only by `turn.lookup`. Pinned in Task 13 (`backgroundedSendRetriesOnlyWithinThirtySeconds`).
4. **A hostile or malformed pair link.** A link with no code, a code of the wrong length, a non-HTTPS non-localhost hub, or arriving when the phone is already paired never enrolls and never sends a request. Pinned in Task 15 (`PairLinkTests`).
5. **A message larger than a client frame** (over about 60 KiB of UTF-8) is refused on the phone before sending, with `protocol_invalid` "This message is too long to send from the phone.", and the draft is kept. It never surfaces as a link error. Pinned in Task 12 (`oversizedTurnIsRefusedBeforeSending`).

### Task 5: Hub watching, live fan-out, and frame limits

**Files:**
- Modify: `Hub/crates/b4hub/src/relay/{link.rs, registry.rs, route.rs}`, `Hub/API.md`
- Test: `Hub/crates/b4hub/tests/relay.rs`

**Interfaces:**
- Produces:
  - **Watchers.** `watchers: host device_id → threadId → set of client device_id`.
  - **`watch`** authorises like `turn.send` (`owner_of_thread` or the pending-thread map). A miss is an `error` frame `not_found`. A fifth watch on one link is an `error` `watch_limit`.

## Execution

I recommend **subagent-driven** execution. There are 16 tasks across two languages, and they meet only at the shared fixtures (`relay.json`, `changes.json`) and at named interfaces. The costly mistakes here (a duplicate turn, a command that runs late, a phone reaching another user's Mac) are exactly what a fresh reviewer per task, checking against the spec, catches before the next task builds on them.

Order:
- Tasks 1 to 5 (the hub) can run before any Swift work except Task 1's Swift half.
- Tasks 6 to 11 are the Mac.
- Tasks 12 to 15 are the phone.
- Task 16 is last.

Two pairs are independent and could run side by side: Tasks 6 and 7 touch disjoint files, and so do Tasks 10 and 12. Everything else is sequential.
